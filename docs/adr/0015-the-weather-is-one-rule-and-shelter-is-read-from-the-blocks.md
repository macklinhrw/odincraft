# The weather is one rule on the island's climate, and shelter is read from the blocks

A dwarf feels the weather, is chilled by it, and is sheltered from it by a roof, by walls and by a fire (`weather.odin`, `shelter.odin`). This is the second half of step P4; the first is ADR 0014.

It is done as the games it is measured by do it, read from their code and data (`interesting-world/notes/colony-sim-reference.md`, section 15), and differs from them where this world is blocks and has a climate of its own.

- **The weather is a function, not a state.** `weather(seconds, stand)` gives the air's warmth, the rain, the wind and the sky at a place so many seconds after the boat stood off. It reads the island's climate (`climate.odin`: the year's mean at the sea's level and how far the months swing from it), the place (its height, the rain that falls there in a year, how it lies to the wind) and the world's seed, and keeps nothing. The dwarves' world asks it of its own tick for where each dwarf stands, and the frame of the same count for the ground under the view: so the rain drawn falling is the rain a dwarf is wet by, and no weather passes between the threads (ADR 0009).
- **The air** is the year's mean, the season's share of the swing (warmest about 1 August, coldest about 30 January), the hour's share of a day's swing of 3.5 °C either way, less under cloud, and 0.65 °C colder each 100 m up. **The sky** is a spell of cloud two days long drawn round the month's mean cover, as before (`SKY`). **Rain** falls in showers of three hours, the likelier the heavier the spell's cloud, for the share of the year's hours that the place's own yearly rain makes at 1.8 mm an hour: so it rains more in the rainy months and more on ground rising into the wind than in its lee, by the figures the island already had. One draw is the whole island's, so the wetter place has the rain whenever the drier has. **The wind** comes from the island's own bearing, stronger under a heavy sky and on ground that faces it, half as strong under a close wood. **Snow** is rain that falls through air of 1 °C or colder; on the start island that is never.
- **The party lands on the first fair morning from the equinox on**, and the spells are counted from that day (`weather_origin`). The frame's sky was already chosen so; now it is one rule for both.
- **Shelter is read from the blocks by one rule** (`shelter_of`), asked by the hold of its own ground and by the frame of the blocks it draws, as an order's cells are (ADR 0011). **A roof** is a block of ground or a block laid, anywhere within sixteen over a dwarf's head: a room dug into a hill has one, a floor laid over walls is one, a tree's crown is not. **A wall** shuts one of the four ways out where a block stands at a dwarf's head within five steps; under a roof a way is also shut where the roof runs on that far. **Indoors** is under a roof with three ways or more shut, so one way may be the door.
- **Cold is a fourth row of `WANTS`.** It falls by what a dwarf feels: the air, less the wind's chill on what stands open to it (0.4 °C for each m/s, by quarters as walls shut the ways), less 5 °C for being wet through, which also doubles the wind's chill. Under 12 °C felt it falls, a quarter of the whole a day for each degree; over that it comes back, and four times as fast by a fire. With none left, in cold under 6 °C felt, a dwarf is harmed, and dies after a day and a half of such hours. Indoors the air is halfway from the air outside to the year's mean, and there is no wind. A dwarf gets wet in rain with no roof over it and dries in three hours, or in half an hour by a fire.
- **A cold dwarf warms itself without being told**, as a thirsty one drinks: by the nearest fire that burns, or in a sleeping place or within a short walk where it would be comfortable dry. With neither it says so, once, and is counted in an alert.
- **A place to sleep is ground the player marks, kept as a stockpile is** (`Stockpile.sleep`): dragged by the same rule, joined to one beside it, taken in the hand whole, made larger, trimmed and removed by the same orders. At night a dwarf lies down in one, under its roof first; with none, or none it has a way to, under a roof or by a fire within a short walk; and with neither, on dry ground where it is.
- **A fire is a thing with its own state** (`Fire`): laid of one log by the work that builds, at a cell the player marks, then fed by whoever has nothing more pressing from the nearest timber wherever it lies. It holds six logs and burns three a day, three times as fast in rain with no roof over it, and goes out with its last. It warms two steps round it in the open and four where it stands indoors, and dries those by it. The player can let it go out, and can remove it. It does not spread.
- **The work that is the remedy is not left for the need** (ADR 0014): laying a fire and feeding one are not left for cold; a fire about to go out is fed, and the log a fire is to be laid of is brought, before any marked work.

We chose a function over a simulated weather kept tick by tick, because the hold and the frame must agree without sharing anything, a run with no window must give the same weather as one with, and a function can be asked for yesterday (the dawn's word) and for the season ahead (start-up prints it). We chose a rule on the blocks round a cell over rooms kept and flooded, as RimWorld keeps them: a room needs a door to close it, which this game has not; a rule of a few dozen block reads is the same on both threads; and a block changed changes nothing but itself. We chose to make the wet and the wind the killers and not the air alone, because the start island's coldest month is 9.8 °C: a real night of rain and wind at that warmth kills, and a dry still one does not.

## Consequences

- **The dwarves' world stays deterministic.** The weather reads the seed, the island's record and the tick. Shelter reads the hold's own ground. The test plays a room marked to sleep in with a fire in it into the second day three ways and compares the logs.
- **Where a dwarf has stood, the hold keeps its blocks** (`walk_block`): a year with no orders keeps 51 chunks' blocks in 7 MB, where the first half kept 48.
- **A tick costs more**: 6 to 8 µs for seven through a year, against 3 to 5: each dwarf asks the weather once a second, and its shelter when it has moved.
- **Left alone the party now dies in its first winter**, of cold, on the 5th and 6th of winter on the start island, after warnings from the 1st. That answers the first half's question whether neglect should kill.
- **What the frame knows is in `Shown`**: each dwarf's warmth, how wet it is, what it feels and how it is sheltered; the fires; and of each sleeping place how many of its places are under a roof and how many indoors.
- **The map of marked blocks holds two bytes a block** (`marks_bind`): what it is marked as, now up to 255 kinds, and which of the blocks beside it are open air. Sleeping places and the shelter lens are kinds on it.
- **The Build tool has Roof and Fire.** Roof lays one layer over the level the drag began on, so begun on a wall's top it lies on the walls; a block on the top of a wall two high is laid from the ground beside it.
- **Left for later**: clothes, which would move what is comfortable; beds and doors (P5); snow that lies; wind that follows the day; storms that harm (N6); fire that spreads; a dwarf slowed or sickened by cold; mood at a night in the rain (P8).
