# Goods are things that lie in cells, and what the hold has is what lies in its stockpiles

What the dwarves' work yields is a thing in the world (`goods.odin`): a dug block of rock is a stone lying where it was dug, a felled tree is logs lying where it stood. The player marks ground for them, a stockpile, and the dwarves carry them there. What the hold has is what lies in its stockpiles, and nothing else is counted. Building takes real things from where they lie. This reverses P3, which counted what was dug into a store with no place, and the plan, which had put carrying off to P5: the owner, playing it, said "i dont define a region where materials i gathered, it goes who knows where and i cant see it" (`open-decisions.md`, 2026-11-16).

It is done as the games it is measured by do it, read from their code and data (`interesting-world/notes/colony-sim-reference.md`, sections 6 and 7).

- **A thing lies in a cell**, the cell a dwarf's feet would be in, with others of its kind up to a heap of eight; RimWorld stacks a resource in a cell (`stackLimit`), Ingnomia ten stones (`StackSize`). Stone is kept by the kind of rock it is, timber by its tree. A heap that overflows spreads to the cells beside it. A dwarf walks through a cell with things in it, as a pawn does.
- **A stockpile is cells of ground with what it takes** (everything, stone or timber): the places a dwarf can stand in the columns dragged over, near the level the drag began on, so it follows a slope (`zone_cells`). Dragging beside one that takes the same makes it larger; Remove takes ground out of it, and what lies there then lies loose. RimWorld's `Zone_Stockpile` is cells with a filter and a priority; there is no priority here yet, since every stockpile is as good as another.
- **Carrying arises from things that do not lie in a stockpile that takes them**, as RimWorld's `ListerHaulables` holds what is not in its best storage. A dwarf with no marked work it can take looks for the nearest such heap, and takes up to four of it to the cell of a stockpile nearest the heap that takes them and has room (`store_cell`; `StoreUtility` takes the nearest good cell of the best zone). The heap's things and the cell's room are promised to it, so two dwarves do not fetch the same stone. Marked work comes first: carrying is what a dwarf does when nothing more pressing can be taken.
- **A block marked to be laid has three states**, as RimWorld's blueprint, frame and building, and Ingnomia's pending, hauling and ready. Marked, it waits for its stuff. A dwarf with nothing more pressing fetches the nearest stone or timber, wherever it lies, stored or loose (`WorkGiver_ConstructDeliverResources` takes the closest reachable thing), and brings it to the order, where it lies as the order's own (`Site.brought`). Then the block is laid, of that very thing: a wall built of basalt dug from the room is basalt. One thing makes one block, as in Ingnomia.
- **A block the dwarves laid remembers it** (`Hold.built`), as Dwarf Fortress's construction record and Ingnomia's `WT_CONSTRUCTED` do. A click on it says "wall of basalt", and Remove on it marks it to be taken down, which gives the thing back where it stood.

We chose things in cells over a count with a place attached (a stockpile as a number) because the owner asked to see what was gathered, and a thing that can be seen can be fetched, run short and be in the way, which is where the stories of such a game come from. We chose one thing a block over RimWorld's five of a material a wall and its one stone chunk in four mined: the world is blocks, and a block dug is a block to build with.

## Friends do not block one another

Carrying fills the ways with dwarves. A dwarf now takes up a floor only where it stands still: a walker goes by whoever is on its way, and takes a floor of its own when it comes to rest, the nearest free one if another has come to stand where it meant to. This is what RimWorld and Ingnomia do (the reference note's takeaway 15). P2 had walkers go round each other and squeeze past, changing floors, after the owner found them stuck on each other; that cost a working face dearly (the same room took 97 to 215 s by when its order fell), and the owner asked for the real games' answer. A way is still a little longer over a floor someone stands on, so a walker goes round a stander where there is room.

## Consequences

- **The dwarves' world stays deterministic** (ADR 0009). The goods are the hold's own. Where a choice is made over a map of cells, which keeps no order, it is made by a rule that gives the same answer in any order (`store_cell` breaks ties by the cell).
- What the frame knows is in `Shown`: every heap, the stockpiles as boxes of their ground, the count of what is stored and of what lies loose, and what each dwarf carries. A block's having been built is told with the block's change.
- **The stores at the top right say what is stored, and say what lies loose is not counted.** The Build tool counts all that lies anywhere, since a wall is built of the nearest.
- A heap on a block that is dug away falls with the ground; one in a cell where a block is laid is moved beside it. What was brought to an order that is taken back lies where the order was.
- **Remove does one of three things**, the first that applies where it is dragged: takes marks back; with none there, takes built blocks down; with none, takes ground out of a stockpile. Its line says which before the button is let go.
- The hold's test (`hold_test.odin`) marks a stockpile, and asks that what was dug is carried to it and that the room is roofed and walled of real things.
- **Left for later**: a priority among stockpiles and a finer filter (by the kind of rock, by the tree); a haul order for one thing; wheelbarrows and how much a dwarf can lift; things that spoil or burn; and goods on disk (P6).
