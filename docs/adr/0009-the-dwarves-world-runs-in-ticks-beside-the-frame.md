# The dwarves' world runs in ticks beside the frame, from the seed and a list of orders

The dwarves' world is a simulation of its own (`hold.odin`). It advances in ticks of a tenth of a second. Everything it is lies in one record, `Hold`: the party, where each dwarf is in its step, the ground it has read, the orders and the log. One thread runs it and nothing else touches that record. The same seed and the same orders, each with the tick it is obeyed at, give the same log every time, with a window or without one (`plan.md`, section 6).

The frame thread and the hold pass three things, under one mutex the frame only tries:

- **Orders in.** The frame appends to an inbox (`hold_send`, `hold_block`). The hold takes them at the start of its next tick, stamps them with that tick and writes them in the log in the form an orders file is read in. So the log of a played game is itself an orders file, and playing it again gives the same events.
- **A picture out.** After each tick the hold lays out `Shown`: who every dwarf is, where it and the boat stood at that tick and at the one before, and what each was doing. The frame copies it and draws between the two. Everything the frame knows of the dwarves is in that copy.
- **The clock.** The game has one clock (ADR 0008). Each frame the hold's clock moves on by the world's time that passed, none while paused and more at a speed, and the frame says which tick it has reached. The hold runs up to it and waits. A hold busy finding a way falls behind, and its clock is then held to two ticks past what was shown: the dwarves' time stretches, and they are never hurried after.

With no window (`--dwarves=seconds --orders=file`) the same thread runs flat out to the time asked and the log is printed. A picture (`--shot`) is of one moment: the hold is run to `--dwarves` and does not move while the frame is drawn.

We chose a thread of its own over stepping the hold on the frame thread, as the water is stepped (ADR 0004): finding a way across 500 m takes 0.2 to 0.5 s when the ground has not been read before, forty frames' worth. We chose ticks stamped on orders over letting the hold read the clock itself, which would make what happens depend on how fast frames come: the clock only says how far the hold may run. We chose a record with one owner over state reached through names bound per thread (ADR 0005), which exists to let many threads read one island: here one thread writes.

## How it learns the ground

Through the generator, as everything does (ADR 0001): a chunk's blocks are made by `gen_columns` and `build_volume`, the calls the world's workers make, with the session's edits laid over them, and read once into a patch of floors and the steps between them (`paths.odin`). The hold never reads the world the frame streams, which holds only what is near the camera and loads in an order that depends on the machine. It keeps its own list of the session's edits, each of which came to it as an order with a tick.

It never reads the simulated water (ADR 0004) either: a dwarf wades by the level the land gives a column, not by the depth standing there in this frame. The land wets only the columns it made wet, so a pit dug in dry ground is dry to a dwarf, as it is in the world drawn.

## Floors are taken up, and a floor can go

A dwarf takes up the floor it stands still on, and no other comes to rest there. **A walker goes by whoever is on its way** and takes up no floor until it stands: friends do not block one another, as in RimWorld and Ingnomia (ADR 0012, "Friends do not block one another"). Its way is a little longer over a floor someone stands on, so it goes round a stander where there is room; and where another has come to stand on the floor it meant to end on, it ends on the nearest free one. The dwarves act in their order in the party, so the same orders still give the same log. P2 first had walkers follow, step round and squeeze past each other, changing floors, which no two could do without one being set back; with dwarves carrying along every way that cost more than it showed.

A changed block may take a dwarf's floor. The dwarf then falls to the first ground below, or is moved out of a block laid where it stood to the nearest free floor, and takes orders again where it comes to rest. One with no floor near stays as it is, says so, and is settled again whenever a block changes or an order comes. An order is asked of one dwarf from each stretch of joined ground the party stands on.

## Consequences

- A change to a block reaches the hold as an order, `block x y z Material`, sent from `world_set`. Anything that changes blocks by another road is invisible to the dwarves. A block order that came from a file and not from the frame is passed out to the frame, which changes the world it draws.
- A dwarf can wade water the simulation has since drained, and walk dry through a pit the player has flooded. A fall does not hurt until dwarves can be hurt (`plan.md`, P4). The two agree wherever nothing has been dug.
- Whatever the hold prints with a time in front is in the log and its hash. Timings are not: they are printed in brackets without a time, and are not the same twice.
- The hold's ground is read chunk by chunk and never let go: about 39 KB a chunk walked or searched, 8.2 MB for the 210 chunks of the run in `plan.md`. A chunk with a changed block keeps its blocks as well, and its floors are read again from them into the places they had (ADR 0011).
- The frame draws a dwarf one tick late, a tenth of a second, so that it always has two ticks to draw between.
- `./build.sh test` plays a game on the start island a stretch at a time, giving orders from where the dwarves have come to, then plays its orders again from a file, alone and beside a thread that reads the hold and moves its clock in fits, and compares the three logs. It keeps no golden hash: what the dwarves do follows the land, which changes on purpose. It runs inside the forming test, because the test runner frees what a test made when the test ends, so only one test can form the world.
- **Nothing counts on seven.** The party, the dwarves and what is shown are lists of any length, and who a dwarf is travels in `Shown` with where it stands, so one can join or die without the frame reading the hold's own record. A tick costs about 10 ns a dwarf (1.0 µs for a hundred, measured). The cost that grows is the looking for ways: an order is tried by one dwarf, which reads the ground the others will cross, and each of the others looks when its turn in the file comes, 0.8 s after the one before, so the searches never fall in one tick.
- Dwarves pause and run at the clock's speeds with everything else, and the log is the same at any speed.
- The log is the hold's own and the frame cannot read it. Each new line is told to the screen as a message (`foundations/interface.md`, I5), with where it happened and which dwarf it is about (`about`), so that a click on it leads there.
- What is wrong with where a dwarf stands travels in `Shown` as its plight (`plight_of`): no ground, shut in, or no way. The screen makes a row of each and adds none of its own knowledge of the ground. Asking it makes no patch and changes no log.
- Selecting is the frame's alone. The hold does not know which dwarf is in the hand.
- **What a hold is started with is said in its log too** (step E-1, `plan.md`). An expedition planned before play chooses a shore and a draw of the seed's party (`Expedition`, `hold.odin`). The hold is made with them, and at its first tick says each in the form an orders file takes: `land x z`, the column of the world the boat is brought in nearest, and `party n i ..`, how often the whole party was drawn again and then which of its dwarves. An orders file with those lines makes the same hold, so a played game's log still plays again. With nothing chosen neither line is said, and the hold is the one the game always made. The party is a function of the seed, its count and those draws (`party_draw`); the landing is asked of the hold's own ground by one rule (`landing_near`), which the planning screen asks of a walk of its own to show where the boat will come in.
