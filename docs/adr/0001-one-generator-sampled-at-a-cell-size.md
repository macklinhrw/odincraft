# One generator, sampled at a cell size

The engine asks the generator a single question: what is the ground at (x, z) when seen at a cell size, from 1 m beside the player to 256 m at the horizon (64 m until 2026-11-14). Detail finer than the cell fades out inside the generator, so a coarse answer is the average of the fine one and every level of detail shows the same world. We chose this over generating at full detail and simplifying meshes afterwards, which earlier attempts did, because it makes a new generator a one-file change and removes the far-terrain bugs that came from sampling fine terrain sparsely.

## Consequences

- Only the nearest ring of chunks is a true block volume. Farther rings are block columns: one height and one surface material per cell, with heights still in whole metres. Overhangs, caves and player edits are therefore invisible beyond about 256 m. When that matters, the far rings need a second source of truth for volume features. Since step 14 (2026-11-12) the roofed places have one: each keeps its outline, and a far column at cells of 2 to 8 m darkens its risers between the floor and roof of any roofed metre in its cell, light and sun alike, so a notch or a shelter's mouth is a dark band from afar.
- Which levels exist follows the camera's distance from the ground, not only its place on the map (2026-11-12): a level is built and drawn only while some ground or sea lies within its ring's reach of the camera, so from 700 m up there is no block volume and no 2 m level.
- A generator that cannot answer at a coarse cell size directly, such as a simulation stored on a grid, has to supply its own averaging.
- Trees follow the same rule by hand: solid blocks in the near volume, single crowns out to 1 km, a statistical canopy beyond. A single crown is a box of leaves hung over the ground on a trunk a metre wide, beside the columns and not part of them; it was first a column of leaves from the ground up, which read as a green pillar (changed 2026-11-12). The canopy is still part of the columns.
- Ground plants (step 9, 2026-11-12) exist only in the nearest ring: crossed pictures over the near volume's columns, drawn from a second buffer on each chunk and thinning out from 70 to 190 m, each sinking into the ground at its own distance. Beyond them the ground's tile is their colour; no far level draws a plant, so a far grassland is flat.
- A column that stands in for something finer, such as the canopy, has open air beside it at the next level in. Where two levels meet, every column edge is therefore closed down to below the ground, not just below its neighbour's top, and the sides of a crown are drawn whole.
- Coarser levels for views across water (step 19, 2026-11-14): 128 and 256 m, so the rings reach about 33 and 65 km. The land's averaged grids go on to 256 m to answer them (`land.z`), and a column wider than 64 m (`COARSE`, `gen.odin`) is that mean alone: a stream or a stack is under a pixel wide 16 km off, and cut or raised at the width of a 256 m cell it would have gouged the far coast or stood a 256 m tower in the sea. Such a column under the sea has no faces, since the sea is opaque that far off, and a chunk of them wholly outside the region is not worked out at all (`open_sea`), so the open sea in the far rings costs nothing; when the world holds many regions, that test becomes whether any region lies under the chunk. In every far ring a run of level, alike tops along a row is one face. The world is drawn round from the same step (`0005`), which is what makes rings this far worth having: a flat world would show a 300 m island whole at any range.
