# The view from above cuts the world at a level

The hold is seen and ordered from above (`above.odin`), and the world can be cut at a height: nothing from that level up is drawn, so the ground under the trees and the inside of a hill show, one level at a time, as Dwarf Fortress shows a world with height. The cut is made where things are drawn, not where they are meshed. Every vertex shader of the view gives the rasteriser a clip distance to the level (`cut_corner`, `shaders.metal`), and where that opens solid blocks a cap closes it: the top of each block under the level that has a block over it, of its own material, meshed on the frame thread from the block volume into one buffer whenever the level or the volume changes (`caps_make`, `render.odin`). So moving the level a block costs one pass over a layer of the volume, about 0.7 ms, and no chunk is meshed again. We chose this over meshing each chunk for the level, which builds every chunk in view again at each step of the level, and over a cap found by counting faces in a stencil, which needs closed meshes and ours draw only the faces that can be seen.

## Consequences

- **A new vertex shader for the view must clip at the level** (`f.cut.x`, the level from the eye), as it must call `drawn`. A shadow pass clips too: what the cut takes away casts no shadow, and a face under a roof is lit as if the roof were gone, since the cut has taken it.
- **Beyond the block volume the cut shows no rock.** The far rings are columns (`0001`), with no inside. A column that stands over the level is lowered to it in the vertex shader, which keeps the far mesh closed at no cost, and its top is drawn in one plain dark tone: the beds show only within about 256 m of the view's middle. A canopy that is part of a column cannot be lifted off it, so where the cut passes through the canopy the column takes the ground's material (kept beside the leaf's in each quad) and stands level with the cut, up to a tree's height too high. A crown hung over the columns is clipped as a block is.
- **A hold's ground is shown cut in blocks while the picture is under about 320 m across** at the usual tilt (450 m straight down, 220 m at the lowest tilt), since everything in the picture then lies within the volume. Wider, the middle is blocks and the edges are columns; past about 800 m across there is no block volume at all. A volume reaching 384 m doubled the blocks kept and the faces meshed and broke the frame target while the view moved (`plan.md`, "P1, as built"), so the reach stays.
- **Each nearest-level chunk keeps what lies under its columns** (`Chunk.under`, 18 KB), because the layers it keeps stop 4 m under its lowest ground and a cut deeper than that still has to show the beds.
- **A quad's last two bytes are no longer spare**: they hold the ground's material under a canopy, and the high byte of its info holds what the cut needs to know of it (`mesh.odin`).
- **Picking goes through the cut** (`pick`, `above.odin`): a ray from over the level begins where it comes down through it, so the block marked is the one seen. Orders to dig and build are to be given through that one procedure.
- **Leaves can be left out** as well, everywhere and at any level: on steep ground a level cut bares only a strip a few trees wide, and below it the wood is whole. Without leaves the ground shows under the whole wood, with the stems standing on it.
- **The shadow maps and the ground plants are centred on the ground under the view's middle**, not on an eye that may stand a kilometre off, and the rings of chunks are centred over that point as high as a first-person eye would see it as large. The cloud layer and its shade are not drawn from above.
