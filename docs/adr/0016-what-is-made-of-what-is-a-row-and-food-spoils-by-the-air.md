# What is made of what is a row, a bench is a thing in a cell, and food spoils by the air it lies in

Food spoils, and the hold makes food that keeps (`goods.odin`, `bench.odin`, `food.odin`). The drying rack is the first thing in the game at which one thing is made into another. This is the step "Winter's food", between P4 and P5 (`plan.md`); the needs and the food it stands on are ADR 0014, the weather ADR 0015, things that lie ADR 0012.

It is done as the games it is measured by do it, read from their code and data (`interesting-world/notes/colony-sim-reference.md`, section 16), and differs where this island's climate asks.

- **A food keeps its row's days** (`FOODS.keeps`), counted in air of 20 °C. A heap ages by the air where it lies, asked of the weather (ADR 0015) every ten seconds: twice as fast for each ten degrees warmer, half as fast for each ten colder, not at all at freezing (`spoil_rate`). Indoors the air is halfway to the year's mean, as a dwarf feels it there. A heap is as old as its things together; when its days are up it is gone, and the hold says what went, the stockpiles' apart from what lay loose, an hour's worth in a line.
- **Making is two tables.** `MAKES` says what is made of what: what goes in, what comes of it, how many of the one make how many of the other, the bench, and the days it stands there. `BENCHES` says what a bench is: the choice of Build that orders it, what it is built of, how much it holds, and what speeds or stops what stands on it (rain, a fire beside it). What comes of a row is itself a row of `FOODS`, whose source is `Made`.
- **A bench is a thing in a cell with its own state** (`Bench`), as a fire is (ADR 0015): ordered with Build, built of the thing brought to it by the work that builds, no block. It holds one row's things at a time, with how far they have come. Dwarves bring it what it makes something of, the heap that will spoil soonest first, and take off what it has made, to a stockpile that takes it or, with none, to its foot. The player can let it stand empty, and can take it down; what is on it and what it was built of then lie there.
- **The one who takes food carries it in.** A gatherer or a fisher keeps what it takes in its arms, an armful of one kind, takes more of the same from within a few steps, and then carries it to a rack that has room or to a stockpile that takes food (`basket_home`). With neither it lays it down where it stands. What the hungry take for themselves still lies at their feet.
- **What is seen to before other marked work** (`fire_first`): a fire about to go out; the stuff a thing is to be built of, whatever the thing; and a rack, unloaded when it is done and loaded while it has room.
- **Dwarves eat what will spoil before what keeps** (`food_find`): kept food counts as lying 120 steps farther off.
- **The lean season is one rule on the island's own food rows** (`lean_of`): a season in which the land within a walk of where the party gathered bears less than twice what those alive eat. It is said a season ahead and while it lasts, against the days of kept food in the stockpiles: at dawn three times a season, and as an alert that stands while the store falls short.
- **A need that is urgent slows the hands** by its row's share (`WANTS.slows`), a fifth each for thirst, hunger and cold, to a half at most.

We chose rows over a procedure for the rack because P5's workshops are the same thing with more rows: a kiln or a carpenter's bench is a row of `BENCHES`, and a plank or a pot a row of `MAKES`. What P5 adds is a second way for a row to be done: by a dwarf's hands standing at the bench for so many seconds, where the rack's rows are done by standing in the air for so many days. That is one field on `Make` and a kind of work beside those of `WORKS`; and a bench that makes more than one thing needs the player's choice of which, a bill, where the rack's rows are told apart by what is laid on it. Stone, timber and metal go through the same haul that fish and shoots do (`bench_load`, `bench_unload`).

We chose food that spoils wherever it lies, as RimWorld's does, over food that is safe in a stockpile, as Dwarf Fortress's is, because a stockpile that stopped rot would leave nothing for a rack to do. We chose a pace that follows the warmth all the way over RimWorld's, which is flat from 10 °C up: this island's winter is 7 to 14 °C, and with RimWorld's rule its cold would keep nothing longer than its spring does.

We chose to have the gatherer carry over leaving a harvest where it was cut for the haulers, as both games do, because carrying here is the last thing a dwarf does: played, a spring's gathering lay in the field and spoiled there while everyone went on gathering. We chose to put a rack's tending and a building's stuff before other marked work for the same reason, each found by playing a year: racks ordered on the second day stood unbuilt until the thirteenth, and then stood done and unloaded while the stockpile's shoots spoiled.

## Consequences

- **The dwarves' world stays deterministic** (ADR 0009). Spoiling reads the weather, which is a function of the tick; benches are the hold's own; where one heap or rack is chosen of several, ties fall to the order the hold keeps them in, which the same orders make the same. The test orders a rack, plays into the fifth day three ways and compares the logs.
- **Nothing is counted while it stands on a bench.** The stores count what lies in the stockpiles (ADR 0012); the rack's card says what is on it.
- **What the frame knows is in `Shown`**: each heap's days left as it lies; each rack and what stands on it; the days of food in store and, of them, the days of food that keeps; how many things are about to spoil; and the lean season with its figures.
- **A year costs a little more**: every ten seconds each heap of food asks the weather, and each rack its roof, its rain and its fire. A year with no orders still runs in about 10 s.
- **Six materials that are no blocks are added** after the party's provisions (`Dried_Bracken` to `Dried_Goby`). The land's own materials keep their figures, so forming is unchanged.
- **Carrying is no longer only for idle hands.** A dwarf at marked work to gather or to fish walks to the stockpile between armfuls, so a gathering takes longer than the marks alone did; and a wall's stone is fetched before a dig beside it is gone on with.
- **Left for later**: a bill that says what a bench makes and how much; work done by hands at a bench (P5); salt, smoke as a thing of its own, cooking and meals; a finer filter for a stockpile (kept food apart from fresh); kept food that is spoiled by rain; a cellar dug for its cool, which the rule already rewards and nothing yet says; and vermin.
