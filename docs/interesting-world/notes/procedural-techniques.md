# Procedural cliffs, rock structure, caves and overhangs: what fits OdinCraft v5

Research notes, 2026-11-11. Every factual claim carries its source. Anything marked **(inference)** is my judgment for this project, not a claim from a source. Section 7 lists what I could not open or verify.

## Key takeaways

1. **The strongest published route to cliffs is a 3D erodibility field inside the stream-power simulation, with a talus angle per rock layer.** Cordonnier et al. 2018 give each point on the eroded surface a rock layer from its original height in a layered, folded crust. Weak layers use a high erosion constant and a 30° landslide slope; hard layers use a low constant and 50°. This yields "cliffs, individual small summits, or circus" ([Cordonnier 2018, §6.2, Fig. 20](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonnier18TVCG.pdf)). OdinCraft's erodibility is smooth 2D noise today, so this is a change to an existing rule, not a new system.
2. **Erodibility contrasts in layered rock never settle into a steady state, and the results can run against intuition.** Forte, Yanites and Whipple 2016 found that stratigraphic order, the orientation of the contact and its dip all change the landscape, and that steady-state denudation is unlikely with strong contrasts in horizontal to moderately tilted layers ([LSU repository abstract](https://repository.lsu.edu/geo_pubs/1266)). Contacts migrate, and channels can be steeper in the weaker rock ([Mitchell & Yanites 2021, abstract and intro](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/9/723/2021/esurf-9-723-2021.pdf)). This transience is what produces knickpoints and waterfalls. A long run to equilibrium would erase it **(inference)**.
3. **Veloren, the one shipped game with a FastScape-style simulation, still makes its cliffs at block scale with noise.** It cuts a sawtooth by taking altitude plus noise modulo 200 (`rem_euclid(200.0)`), and its mesas use `fract(alt/scale)` steps ([column.rs](https://gitlab.com/veloren/veloren/-/raw/master/world/src/column.rs)). Cliff zones are traced on the coarse map by walking up the steepest gradient ([sim/mod.rs `generate_cliffs`](https://gitlab.com/veloren/veloren/-/raw/master/world/src/sim/mod.rs)). The simulation gives it rock strength and talus angles; the cliffs themselves are not caused by them. This is the gap OdinCraft can close.
4. **Sub-grid terracing works when it reads the same strata function as the simulation.** Veloren's sawtooth and Gaea's Terrace and Stratify nodes all remap height. If the remap reads the simulation's rock function and applies only where the simulation already made steep ground, the benches line up with the large-scale forms. OdinCraft v4 recorded "declared priors, artificial banding" (`docs/ideas.md`), which is the failure when banding is not tied to cause **(inference)**.
5. **The best published point-query detail for the 16 m to 1 m gap is a gradient-aligned erosion filter.** It is a noise of extruded sine stripes running down the local gradient, cell-pivoted like Worley noise, in octaves that each follow the slope the previous octave left. Every point can be evaluated on its own, and the code is MPL-2.0 ([runevision blog, March 2026](https://blog.runevision.com/2026/03/)). Its stated weak point is telling valleys from ridges on altitude alone. OdinCraft has drainage area, which answers that directly **(inference)**.
6. **Overhangs, undercuts and sea caves can come from one subtraction.** Paris et al. 2019 subtract `f_D·(1−ρ(p))` from the field, where ρ is a 3D rock-resistance function. Erosion intensity is `e(ρ,σ) = σ(1−ρ)(1−β) + σβ`, with stress σ set to `A^½·s` for rivers and to a band around sea level for coasts ([Paris 2019, §5.3, §6.1](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~Jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/paris2019.pdf)). In OdinCraft's 256 m voxel volume this becomes a density term, not a construction tree **(inference)**.
7. **Cave networks that follow geology are a graph problem, cheap at island scale.** Paris et al. 2021 run A* over Poisson samples that are dense on inception horizons, the water table and permeable volumes, connected to 32 nearest neighbours. The cost weighs distance to a horizon, alignment with fractures and permeability. Sinks are inlets and springs are outlets, and building the graph takes under a second ([Paris PhD thesis, ch. 7](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/phd/axel-paris-phd-thesis.pdf)).
8. **Thermal erosion and landslides give talus and scars at almost no cost.** Schott's shipped shader has a per-cell repose test with a noise-varied threshold of tan 0.9 to 1.4 ([thermal.glsl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/H-Schott/MultiScaleErosion/HEAD/data/shaders/thermal.glsl)). HyLands adds Culmann landslides: a cell is unstable when slope exceeds the friction angle φ, slides with probability `H_s/H_c`, and fails along a plane dipping at `(β+φ)/2` ([Campforts 2020, §2.2](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/13/3863/2020/gmd-13-3863-2020.pdf)).
9. **Real lakes come from keeping the water surface while leaving the ground alone.** Cordonnier, Bovy and Braun 2019 build a graph of basins and a minimum spanning tree over their passes in linear time, and either fill or carve inside depressions ([ESurf 2019](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/7/549/2019/esurf-7-549-2019.pdf)). Veloren uses "exact" Planchon-Darboux filling, "which does not change altitudes", and then connects lakes through passes ([erosion.rs](https://docs.veloren.net/src/veloren_world/sim/erosion.rs.html)).
10. **Dependency order: strata in the simulation, then strata in block materials and sub-grid terracing, then undercuts and caves.** Every later step reads the same rock function, which is what keeps the layers on a cliff face consistent with the cliff's position on the map **(inference)**.

## 1. Geology-aware landscape evolution

### 1.1 Layered and 3D erodibility in stream power

- **Cordonnier et al. 2018, "Sculpting Mountains".** Uses the stream-power law `∂z/∂t = u − k·A^m·s^n` with n = 1 and m = 0.5, solved implicitly in linear time by "evaluating the heights in the right order". It adds a landslide step adapted from thermal erosion: wherever slope exceeds a talus angle, height is updated. Each surface point's rock layer comes from its original height h₀, interpolated between the two nearest folded layer surfaces. Layers are split into sub-layers of different strength: high k and 30° for weak rock, low k and 50° for hard rock. The paper reports cliffs, isolated summits and cirques, and compares one result with the "velodrome" near Digne, a gently folded stack of alternating hard and soft beds. Its stated limitations are a fold model valid only for early folding and no glacial erosion. Speed: 10 ms per iteration on a 10,000-vertex mesh, and 4 s per step on 1 M vertices offline. The renders add slope- and elevation-weighted noise ([PDF, §6–7](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonnier18TVCG.pdf)).
  - **Fit (inference): high.** For OdinCraft, `K(x,z) = K_rock(strata(x, z, h_current))`, with strata given by planes with a strike and dip plus a low-frequency warp for folds. That is one function call per cell per step. The cost is negligible against the 1 s budget. One caveat: at 16 m cells, beds thinner than about 30–50 m will not show in the simulation, so finer beds must come from the sub-grid step (§5).
- **Forte, Yanites & Whipple 2016** ran the CHILD model with two units and varied the erodibility contrast, the order of the units and the dip of the contact. Stratigraphic order, contact orientation and dip all mattered, and there was no simple relation between erodibility and erosion rate ([abstract via LSU](https://repository.lsu.edu/geo_pubs/1266)). I did not open the full text.
- **Mitchell & Yanites 2021** used 1-D stream-power models through dipping layers. Contact migration follows kinematic wave speed, and weaker units can carry steeper channels ([ESurf 2021](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/9/723/2021/esurf-9-723-2021.pdf)). **Inference:** dip direction relative to flow decides whether a hard bed forms a waterfall that retreats upstream or one that holds still. Expect cuestas and hogbacks from dipping beds, without designing them.
- **FastScape** takes `kf` as a full `nx*ny` array, so erodibility can vary per cell. Its DippingDyke example shows "spatially and temporally variable erodibility" ([fastscapelib-fortran docs](https://fastscape.org/fastscapelib-fortran/)). The docs describe Braun & Willett 2013 as "a very efficient, O(n), implicit and parallel method" (same page).
- **Veloren erosion.rs** derives a per-cell maximum slope of 15°–60° from a 3D rock-strength noise. Erodibility `kf` is multiplied by 4 on sediment cells. It uses ADI hillslope diffusion and soil production from Dietrich et al. 2003, and a "hacky version of thermal erosion" that looks only at the lowest neighbour ([erosion.rs](https://docs.veloren.net/src/veloren_world/sim/erosion.rs.html)). Rock strength is sampled in 3D at a height of `uheight·mountain_scale` ([sim/mod.rs](https://gitlab.com/veloren/veloren/-/raw/master/world/src/sim/mod.rs)), so it is a 3D field indexed by uplift, not by the current surface. Devblog 43 says thermal erosion counters stream power's "tendency... to create extremely steep slopes" ([devblog 43](https://veloren.net/devblog-43)).

### 1.2 Hillslope laws that keep edges sharp

- **Roering, Kirchner & Dietrich 1999.** Flux is nearly linear at low gradient and rises steeply toward a critical gradient. The result is convex divides with planar slopes below them ([abstract](https://zendy.io/title/10.1029/1998wr900090)). I read only the abstract and did not verify the exact equation. Veloren cites Perron 2011 for its nonlinear transport (erosion.rs, above).
  - **Fit (inference): medium.** It makes ridges straighter and narrower than linear creep, which is part of why OdinCraft's ridges are rounded. A cheaper move is to lower linear creep on hard rock.
- **HyLands (Campforts 2020).** Unstable cells are those with slope above φ. Each one slides with probability `p = H_s/H_c`, where `H_c = 4C·sinβ·cosφ / (ρg·(1−cos(β−φ)))`. The failure plane dips at `(β+φ)/2` and spreads upslope by a stack flood. Debris runs out downslope. Iterations take 0.02–6.5 s on Landlab test grids ([GMD 2020, §2.2, Table](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/13/3863/2020/gmd-13-3863-2020.pdf)). The authors note that very small simulated landslides are overpredicted in volume (same paper).
  - **Fit (inference): medium-high as one late pass.** It produces scarps and fans, with cohesion C read from the rock function. It is O(cells), plus a small flood per slide.
- **Thermal and talus.** Schott's GPU thermal pass moves material between neighbours when the height difference over distance exceeds a threshold, and noise varies the threshold ([thermal.glsl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/H-Schott/MultiScaleErosion/HEAD/data/shaders/thermal.glsl)). This is the classic Musgrave-style rule. I did not open Musgrave 1989 itself.

### 1.3 Lakes, deposition, coasts and glaciers

- **Lakes.** See takeaway 9. Cordonnier 2019 also notes that the 2016 graphics implementation had sub-optimal complexity ([ESurf 2019](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/7/549/2019/esurf-7-549-2019.pdf)). **Fit (inference): high.** OdinCraft already priority-floods. The change is to keep the fill level as a water surface and the unfilled ground as lake bed, and to route through the pass.
- **Sediment deposition (Yuan et al. 2019).** An O(N), implicit, unconditionally stable solution of stream power with a deposition term G ([abstract, ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019JGRF..124.1346Y)). In FastScape, G > 0 needs about 1 to 20 Gauss-Seidel iterations as G approaches 1 ([fastscape docs](https://fastscape.org/fastscapelib-fortran/)). It forms floodplains, fans and lake infill. I read only the abstract.
- **Schott's deposition shader.** Sediment carried in from upstream is deposited when `strength·sed > A^0.3·s²` ([deposition.glsl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/H-Schott/MultiScaleErosion/HEAD/data/shaders/deposition.glsl)). This is a cheap explicit alternative.
- **Coasts.** The only coastal model I opened is Paris 2019's sea-erosion stress: a falloff distance from sea level times coastal accessibility (Miller 1994). Its example limits sea action to ±8 m of sea level, giving "strong overhangs at the base of the cliff", with an arch and a sea cave wherever the rock is weaker ([Paris 2019, Fig. 11, §6.1](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~Jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/paris2019.pdf)). I did not find a geomorphology wave-erosion model with a clear graphics-scale recipe.
- **Glacial erosion (Cordonnier et al. 2023).** A learned estimator of ice flow plus multi-scale advection produces U-shaped valleys, hanging valleys, fjords and glacial lakes, under 5 min for 16 × 16 km ([search summary of the paper; PDF](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Cordonnier23ToG.pdf)). **Fit: low.** It is too heavy, and a 6 km temperate island has no reason for it **(inference)**.

For the fade target use log drainage area or distance to the river instead of altitude, which the author calls "not robust" ([blog](https://blog.runevision.com/2026/03/)) |

| **Multi-scale erosion amplification** (Schott 2024) | A low-resolution heightfield | Grid passes; doubles resolution between rounds of "erosion → thermal → deposition" ([repo README](https://github.com/H-Schott/MultiScaleErosion)) | Consistent dendritic detail | **Medium.** It is a grid pass, not a point query. It could take a few tiles near the player to 4 m and 2 m, but it fights the "one function at every LOD" rule |

Schott's erosion shader, read directly: multiple-flow drainage with slope weighting `pow(s,1.3)`, erosion `k·A^0.8·s^2` clamped, and height never lowered below the receiver's ([erosion.glsl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/H-Schott/MultiScaleErosion/HEAD/data/shaders/erosion.glsl)). Drainage converges over iterations. A `hardness` buffer is bound but not used in the lines I read. I could not open the 2024 paper (HAL returned an access-denied page), so I have the abstract and code only ([project page](https://h-schott.github.io/p/mserosion)).

Veloren's bridge from its 32-block chunks to blocks: altitude uses monotone cubic interpolation (`get_interpolated_monotone`, which cites Steffen 1990). It then adds a "marble" noise warp scaled by `(10 + rockiness·15)·gradient`, faded out within 64 m of water ([column.rs](https://gitlab.com/veloren/veloren/-/raw/master/world/src/column.rs); chunk size 2⁵ in [terrain/mod.rs](https://gitlab.com/veloren/veloren/-/raw/master/common/src/terrain/mod.rs)). **Inference:** monotone interpolation is worth copying, because it does not overshoot at riverbanks and cliff edges.

## 3. True 3D features

- **Arches (Peytavie 2009).** A grid of material-layer stacks (bedrock, rock, sand, air and water) turned into a convolution implicit surface. It includes a stabilisation pass for sand and rock by repose angle and a phenomenological rule for rocks detaching from bedrock into piles ([PDF](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/egalin/Articles/2009-arches.pdf)). **Fit (inference):** the layer-stack idea is close to "height columns plus material runs", which OdinCraft's far field already is.
- **Paris 2019, implicit 3D features.** The geology is a construction tree giving a resistance ρ(p) in [0,1], built from strata, warp, fault and sphere primitives. The heightfield is converted to an implicit field through a sigmoid of vertical distance. The paper describes four effects:
  - Shallow erosion: Poisson-sample a region and place negative spheres with energy `e(ρ,σ)`.
  - Deep erosion (karst): invasion percolation from sinks, always dequeuing the least-resistant candidate, perturbed by ε ≈ 0.1. It carves only where `ρ < ρ₀`.
  - Hoodoos: a grammar driven by drainage area, slope and ρ.
  - Stratified cliffs: the `f − f_D(1−ρ)` node, used for cliffs and floating islands.

  Performance: about 1 M field queries per second with a bounding-volume hierarchy over thousands of primitives. One cave example used 165,773 spheres. A plain noise node at the root "often introduces unwanted holes and disconnected surface components", so they deform the radius of influence anisotropically instead ([PDF §4–7](https://people.cs.uct.ac.za/~Jgain/wp-content/papercite-data/pdf/paris2019.pdf)). The open re-implementation is not the paper's code ([repo](https://github.com/aparis69/Implicit-Volumetric-Terrains)).
  - **Fit (inference):** take the formulas, not the tree. In the voxel band the density is `d(p) = h(x,z) − y − U·soft(p)·stress(x,z)·band(y)`. Here `soft` is 1 − hardness from the strata function, `stress` is high where the simulated slope is near or above the cliff threshold or where `A^½·s` is high beside a channel, and `band` limits the term to a few metres around river or sea level. That gives undercut soft beds, overhanging hard caps and gorge-wall notches, with nothing placed by hand.
- **Paris 2021, cave networks** (read from thesis ch. 7, which reproduces the paper). The inputs are the terrain, an active water table, inception horizons, permeability (a construction tree), fracture orientations, and key points: sinks, springs and waypoints.
  - Sampling: Poisson r = 8 m on horizons and the water table, importance sampling in permeable volumes, then r = 16 m fill elsewhere, connected to the 32 nearest neighbours. Fewer neighbours gives "unrealistic or even unfeasible paths".
  - Cost per edge: `c = w_L·smoothstep(|z − L_z|/r) + w_F·Σ w_k(1 − (n_k·ṗ)²) + w_π·π(p)`.
  - Network: paths between all key points are merged into a network, refined with stochastic midpoint displacement, and tunnels are classed as phreatic tubes, vadose canyon or keyhole, or epikarst shafts by distance to the water table.
  - Timing: the graph builds in under a second, the slowest step takes a few seconds, and geometry is an implicit surface ([thesis](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/phd/axel-paris-phd-thesis.pdf); code without surface generation: [Karst-Synthesis](https://github.com/aparis69/Karst-Synthesis)).
  - **Fit (inference): high for a one-off pass at load.** Inception horizons are just chosen bed contacts in OdinCraft's strata. Sinks are the simulation's closed depressions on soluble rock. Springs are where a horizon outcrops on a valley wall below the sink. A list of capsules can then be tested per voxel.
- **Veloren caves** use a jittered grid of nodes per level (1536 m cells, 5 levels about 120 m apart). Tunnels are quadratic splines between nodes, with radius 8–64 m from noise. Surface entrances are kept away from cliffs, water and slopes with gradient above 0.75 ([layer/cave.rs](https://gitlab.com/veloren/veloren/-/raw/master/world/src/layer/cave.rs)). They are simpler but not geological.
- **Minecraft (Java 1.18+).** Noise caves are part of the base density: "cheese" (large pockets), "spaghetti" (tunnels along noise edges), "noodle" (1–5 blocks) and pillars. Older carvers are 85–112 blocks long. Aquifers use floodedness and spread noises sampled per 16×40×16 cell ([wiki: Cave](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cave)). Overhangs come only from final density varying in y ([wiki: Noise router](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router)). **Inference:** isotropic and geology-blind, which is the "generic" look the project wants to avoid.
- **Vintage Story** stacks rock strata through noise per rock type (`rockstrata.json`, with amplitudes, thresholds and frequencies) under a geologic-province map. A modder reports a fixed order of strata types ([forum](https://www.vintagestory.at/forums/topic/12397-any-worldgen-experts-that-have-messed-with-strata-variety/), [Rock Strata Variety README](https://github.com/chisps427/Rock-Strata-Variety/blob/main/README.md)). These are community sources only.

**Cliffs in 1 m voxels (inference; no source gives a block-specific recipe).** Combine:
- Bed-scale risers and benches from §5.
- Joint sets: two vertical Worley or cell families aligned with the strike and its normal. Each cell's face is offset by a random 0–2 m, so the riser breaks into prisms instead of a smooth extruded wall.
- Undercut soft beds from the Paris density term.
- A talus apron below: a repose-angle fan of loose-rock material whose top is the simulated cliff foot, plus scattered fallen blocks sized by the thickness of the hard bed above.

A staircase reads as a staircase when every riser is the same height and every bench the same width. Bed thickness drawn from a distribution, together with the dip, breaks that up.

## 4. Shipped games and tools

- **Veloren.** See §1.1 and §2. Its block cliffs are a terrace offset, `cliff = |((alt + noise) mod 200)/64 − 1|^1.5 · cliff_height`. `cliff_height` (up to 44) is painted around cells found by walking steepest gradients above 1.5 on the coarse map, and is suppressed near water ([column.rs](https://gitlab.com/veloren/veloren/-/raw/master/world/src/column.rs), [sim/mod.rs](https://gitlab.com/veloren/veloren/-/raw/master/world/src/sim/mod.rs)). ## 5. Sub-grid cliff tricks that respect causes

1. **Strata-terraced height (inference; built from Veloren's `fract` mesa offset and Cordonnier's hardness per sub-layer).** Let `b(p) = dot(p − p0, n̂_bed)` be the bed coordinate, with dip and strike in n̂ and a low-frequency warp for folds. Beds are an aperiodic sequence of thicknesses, each hard or soft. For a column with interpolated simulated height h and slope s, find the bed at h. Push the height toward the top of a hard bed and pull it toward the base of the soft bed above, with weight `w = smoothstep(s_lo, s_hi, s)·hardness_contrast`. The result is a riser at hard beds and a bench on soft ones. Dip makes the benches tilt and the ledges converge along the strike. Cost: a short search over bed boundaries (a precomputed table) plus about 10 operations per point. It fits point queries at every LOD, because the far LOD sees the same function at coarser sampling.
2. **Cliff bands where slope exceeds strength.** Apply the remap only where the simulated slope exceeds the rock's talus angle minus a margin. That is where the simulation itself says rock stands. This follows lesson 62 in `docs/lessons.md` ("Cliffs need bare rock") and makes stone paint caused rather than painted.
3. **Joint-aligned blocks.** See §3. Use the same strike as the beds so the joints match the cuesta trend.
4. **Talus cones.** Below every riser taller than about 3 m, add debris at the repose angle (about 34°), sized by the riser height. The simulation-side equivalents are Schott's thermal pass and the HyLands runout.

## 6. Ranked shortlist for OdinCraft v5 (most visible result per line)

1. **A 3D strata erodibility and talus field in the simulation** (Cordonnier 2018). It replaces the 2D noise K with K and repose angle from dipping beds at the current surface height. Expect benches, cuestas, knickpoints and waterfalls where rivers cross hard beds, and gorges below them. About 40 lines. **Everything below depends on this function.**
2. **The same strata function chooses block materials and the stone/soil split by slope against strength** (§5.2). Cliffs read as layered rock that matches their cause. About 30 lines. Depends on 1.
3. **Strata-terraced sub-grid height** (§5.1, generalising Veloren's mesa offset). Visible ledges at 1–5 m inside 16 m cells. About 40 lines. Depends on 1. Avoid uniform bed spacing, which was v4's "artificial banding".
4. **Lakes kept as water surfaces** (Cordonnier 2019 / Veloren exact fill). Uses the existing priority-flood output, about 30 lines, and needs `water.odin` to accept a lake level.
5. **A gradient-aligned erosion filter on soil-covered slopes**, faded by drainage area (Johansen 2026). Coherent gullies between 16 m and 1 m. About 80 lines. Independent of 1, but should be masked off on rock cliffs.
6. **An undercut density term in the voxel band** (Paris 2019 `f − f_D(1−ρ)`, with stress from slope and channel stream power). Overhangs, notched gorge walls and sea notches. About 30 lines. Depends on 1 and on the near field being a true volume.
7. **Talus and landslide pass** (Schott thermal with a varied angle, then optionally HyLands Culmann). Scree aprons and scars. About 40–80 lines.
8. **A karst graph** (Paris 2021): sinks from closed depressions, springs at horizon outcrops, A* over Poisson samples. Caves with real entrances and resurgences. About 200 lines. Depends on 1 (inception horizons) and 4 (sinks).

Not recommended now (inference): glacial erosion, example-based synthesis, full construction trees, and Minecraft-style isotropic noise caves.

## 7. What I could not verify

- **Cordonnier et al. 2016** (HAL returned an access-denied page).
