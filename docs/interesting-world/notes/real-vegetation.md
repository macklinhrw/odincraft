# What grows on the reference islands

Written 2026-11-12, after the owner asked how the first plant communities compared with real life and found the purple ground odd. The eight islands the land is measured against (`real-islands.md`) lie between about 27° and 34° north. Their plants are the warm-temperate and subtropical evergreen forest of southern Japan. The first cast (beech, katsura, red pine, heather) came from the plan's cool-temperate setting and matched none of them.

Sources are linked. Where a statement is from memory it says so.

## What the sources say

- **Izu Islands (Toshima, Mikurajima, Kōzushima, Aogashima).** Evergreen broadleaf forest of *Castanopsis sieboldii* (shii), *Machilus thunbergii* (tabu) and camellia runs from the shore to about 450 m on Miyakejima ([BirdLife](https://datazone.birdlife.org/site/factsheet/miyakejima-island-iba-japan/text)). Mikurajima has three zones: Castanopsis forest low down, a transitional zone, and a mixed *Buxus*, *Eurya* and *Trochodendron* forest high up, with trees shorter and leaves smaller as height rises ([Vegetation Science 18(2)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/vegsci/18/2/18_KJ00006916479/_article/-char/en)). Toshima splits the same way, a lowland Castanopsis and Persea type and a highland Eurya and Styrax type ([Vegetation Science 18(1)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/vegsci/18/1/18_KJ00006916465/_article/-char/en)). Mikurajima's largest shii has a trunk about 14 m round ([Tokyo parks](https://www.kankyo1.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/naturepark/english/know/park/introduction/kokuritsu/fujihakone/mikurashima/animals_and_plants.html)); the species commonly passes 20 m.
- **Young volcanic ground.** On Miyakejima, bare lava is taken first by *Miscanthus condensatus* (Hachijō susuki, a tall grass), then by alder (*Alnus sieboldiana*) scrub, then deciduous trees, then the evergreen forest ([Forests 12(11)](https://mdpi.com/1999-4907/12/11/1435), [Plants 10(11)](https://mdpi.com/2223-7747/10/11/2500)).
- **How long the forest takes.** From memory, not opened: on Miyakejima's lava flows of known age, alder scrub stands on those 16 and 37 years old, a wood of tabu (*Machilus*) and cherry on the flow of 1874, about 125 years old, and shii (*Castanopsis*) forest on ground 800 years old and more (Kamijo and others, 2002). The slide's plants follow that: susuki, alder, a young tabu wood, then the laurel forest.
- **Wind and the sea.** On Hachijōjima, wind-exposed sea cliffs carry susuki grassland with dwarf bamboo, and sheltered gullies carry evergreen forest ([Kanagawa museum checklist](https://nh.kanagawa-museum.jp/assets/icp/contents/1600215159217/simple/Bull40_7-34_katsuyama.pdf)). On warm-temperate Japanese coasts the zones run inland from the shore: drift line, dune grass, a grass zone of *Miscanthus* and *Farfugium*, scrub of *Pittosporum tobira* and *Eurya emarginata*, then black pine (*Pinus thunbergii*) ([MLIT](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/H30-00998.html), [J. Forest Research](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10310-010-0207-8)).
- **Ogasawara (Hahajima, Chichijima).** Three structures: mesic forest 13 to 20 m tall in moist lowlands with deep soil; dry forest with a closed canopy 3 to 8 m tall on slopes; dry scrub 0.3 to 1.5 m on windy ridges and sea cliffs ([Shimizu & Tabata](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/aa075e61-a32a-41e5-9bd5-458621dc7a33), [Ogasawara subtropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogasawara_subtropical_moist_forests)).
- **Tsushima.** Lowland forest of *Castanopsis*, with *Machilus* lowest; fir and evergreen oak on the hills ([Vegetation Science 25(2)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/vegsci/25/2/25_KJ00005989741/_article/-char/en)).
- **How closed the forest is.** Old shii forest plots hold about 1,400 to 1,500 stems a hectare and 30 to 60 m² of trunk a hectare ([FFPRI bulletin](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ffpri/20/1/20_37/_article/-char/en), [Forests 8(10)](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4907/8/10/397)). From memory: the canopy is closed, the floor is in deep shade under leaf litter with little grass, and there is a layer of shrubs and small trees (camellia, *Eurya*, *Aucuba*) beneath.

Not found: land-cover shares for any of the eight islands, a vegetation survey of Kikaijima (tourism pages say sugar cane covers most of its flat land), and figures for crown width.

## What that changed

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Slope forest of beech | Laurel forest: shii with tabu, camellia beneath | The forest of every reference island below about 450 m |
| Valley wood of katsura | Valley forest of tall tabu, also in deep hollows | Ogasawara's mesic forest and Hachijō's sheltered gullies |
| Ridge pine on red boles | Ridge wood: low small-leaved evergreens (hisakaki) with some pine | Mikurajima's upper zone and Ogasawara's dry forest |
| Scrub and purple heather | Susuki grass, dwarf bamboo and wind-cut bushes | Heather moor is north-west European (from memory); the windy ground of these islands is tall grass and scrub |
| Coast strand of pine and scrub | Black pine and tobira scrub over dune grass, in a belt about 50 m deep | The coastal zones above; the belt was 150 m deep and read as a sandy plain |
| Podzol on dry humps | Red soil there; black ash soil on the volcanic islands | Podzol belongs to cool conifer country (from memory); red and yellow soils and volcanic ash soils are the soils of southern Japan (from memory) |
| Open wood, crowns 5 to 7 m across | Crowns closing over the slopes, and bushes under the trees | The stand figures above |

## Stands

Added 2026-11-12 for step 7, stands with ages. Read from search summaries of the papers, which were not opened; the figures for the six Okinawa plots, the young stands and Yanbaru are tied to the links below by those summaries and were not checked against the papers.

- **Gaps.** In 8.2 ha of primary evergreen broad-leaved forest in western Japan, 161 canopy gaps covered 15.7% of the ground, with a mean of 80 m² (about 10 m across); gaps under 80 m² were much the commonest, over 400 m² were rare, and 79.5% were made by the death or injury of one tree (Yamamoto 1992, [J. Plant Research](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF02489401.pdf), [abstract](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/1001403226)). On a 4 ha plot on Tsushima, struck by a strong typhoon in 1987 and watched from 1990 to 1997, gaps covered 17.1%; they formed at 0.72% of the ground a year and closed at 1.61% (Miura and others 2001; the same plot's [KAKEN report](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/en/report/KAKENHI-PROJECT-02640513/026405131991kenkyu_seika_hokoku_gaiyo/) gives gaps 5 to 20 m across in low *Castanopsis* and *Distylium* forest).
- **Typhoons.** In a *Castanopsis cuspidata* stand at Minamata watched for 49 years, typhoons uprooted or snapped many of the fastest-growing trees ([Dryad](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.tx95x6b3q)). On a 1 ha plot in Miyazaki, stems fell from 1,532 to 1,379 a hectare between 1998 and 2019, and trunk area fell after 2014 as typhoons killed *C. sieboldii* ([figshare](https://figshare.com/articles/Thirty-year_stand_dynamics_of_an_old_evergreen_coppice_forest_in_Southern_Kyushu_Southwestern_Japan/7110758)).
- **Stems by size.** On six old plots in Okinawa (stems of 3 cm and more), 3,875 to 6,625 stems a hectare, falling with each 10 cm of size: 70.5% under 10 cm, 9.9% of 20 cm and more, which held 42 to 67% of 45 to 62 m² a hectare of trunk ([Taiwania](https://taiwania.ntu.edu.tw/download/tai.2008.53.134.pdf/712/issue)). The old warm-temperate plot at Tatera, Tsushima: 4,570 living stems of 5 cm and more on 4 ha (about 1,140 a hectare) and 64 m² a hectare of trunk, with *C. sieboldii* the largest and with few small stems of its own ([population structure](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/888609480)). Read together: some 1,100 to 1,500 stems of 5 cm and more a hectare, of which a tenth or so are canopy trees of 20 cm and more. That puts the canopy trees some 6 to 9 m apart (an inference, not a measured spacing).
- **Young stands.** After clearing, sprouts from the stumps make dense young stands that thin themselves along the −3/2 law ([Okinawa](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11258-025-01538-6)); in Yanbaru, 60 years after logging, *C. sieboldii* stems over 25 cm were fewer than half those of the uncut forest, and the size spread had not recovered ([Japanese Journal of Forest Planning](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjfp/48/1/48_27/_article/-char/en)). No count of stems a hectare at 20 years was found.

**How ours compare** (step 7, the default island, counted over 0.36 ha squares of laurel forest):

| | Real | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| Old stand, canopy trees a hectare | about 100 to 300 of 20 cm and more (inferred) | about 190 |
| Old stand, trees between the canopy and small ones beneath | about 1,000 more of 5 to 20 cm | about 290 between, 240 small trees and bushes |
| Old stand, all stems | 1,100 to 1,500 (5 cm and more) | about 700 |
| Young stand (22 years), stems | thousands of sprouts (not counted) | about 800 canopy, 110 between, 50 small |
| Gaps, share of an old stand | 15.7 to 17.1% | 15% set; a gap 3 to 8 m in radius, mean area about 100 m² against the real 80 |
| Ground under a crown, from above | closed but for gaps (from memory) | 67 to 80% old, 93 to 96% young |

So an old wood has about half the real stems: blocks cannot show a stem thinner than a metre, and each small tree costs faces. A young wood has the dense poles and low even canopy of a regrowing stand. The canopy of an old wood is more open from above than a real one, since crowns are held at 5 to 7 m across and 15 m tall until the tree grower.

## What is still unlike the real thing

- **Tree height.** Real shii and tabu stand 13 to 20 m and more, and the largest far more; ours now stand 13 to 20 m, with none of the giants.
- **One flora.** Hahajima and Chichijima are subtropical, with palms, pandanus and tree ferns; Kikaijima is farmland. A second flora, and land that people have cleared, are later rows.
- **Young volcanic ground** has no grass and alder stage yet. The volcanic kinds are wooded like old islands.
- **Height above the sea changes nothing yet.** The real forest shortens and changes above about 450 m; ours has no rule for height, and the island's top is 350 m.
