# How Minecraft and its realism-minded relatives make worlds varied

Research notes, 2026-11-11. Vanilla numbers come from the current data files (the misode/mcmeta mirror, which is at `26.4-snapshot-8`: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/version.json). Sections marked **(c) inference** are my own judgement about OdinCraft, not something a source says.

## Key takeaways

1. **Vanilla Minecraft varies the world by multiplying a few independent fields.** Terrain shape comes from three 2D noises (continentalness, erosion, peaks-and-valleys). Biome comes from a 6D lookup that adds temperature, humidity and depth. Neither one decides the other (https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation). OdinCraft already has a simulated equivalent of the terrain fields. The part it lacks is the second set: climate and wetness fields that pick the content.
2. **Biomes differ at eye level through a handful of placed decorations, not through terrain.** In vanilla, `plains`, `forest` and `old_growth_spruce_taiga` share about 37 underground and ore features. Each has only about 10–15 surface features of its own: a tree mix, a flower list, a grass patch, mushrooms, berries or ferns, and one signature item such as `forest_rock` boulders (biome JSONs below).
3. **TerraFirmaCraft is the clearest example of "simulate the why, design the what".** A region pipeline annotates plate boundaries, distance to ocean, climate, rock type and rivers. It then picks among 125 named, designed landforms with explicit rules. Hot, wet limestone gets tower karst. Diverging plate edges get rift valleys. Cold areas that were once glaciated get drumlins and knob-and-kettle (`ChooseBiomes.java`).
4. **TFC's trees come with their own floor.** Each species' forest entry carries a climate window plus twigs, fallen leaves, fallen logs, dead trees, bushes and its own soil disc, for example alfisol under oak (`oak_entry.json`). That is a cheap way to make each forest feel different underfoot.
5. **ReTerraForged builds a region map of designed terrain types and blends them in pairs.** It has 9 "mixable" lowland types, which give 36 pairwise blends, plus mountains and volcanoes kept separate. It then runs a droplet erosion filter over the result. The surface is chosen by steepness and height thresholds (rock above slope 0.65, scree above 0.4) (`TerrainProvider.java`, `Preset.java`).
6. **Terralith and Tectonic get cliffs and arches with nothing but density-function math.** Terralith has dedicated `cliff/carve`, `arch`, `spikes`, `dune` and `crater` functions layered onto the vanilla offset spline. Its cliffs are a clamped "max cut" noise that steps the terrain height. This is a designed shape stamped where a 2D mask allows it.
7. **Vintage Story's landforms are pure data.** Each landform is 10 octave amplitudes, octave thresholds and a vertical density profile of y-keys. A sharp jump in the profile makes a cliff, and a flat run at sea level makes a beach. Its trees come from a data-defined recursive branch grower, with species picked by temperature, rain, fertility, forestation and elevation windows.
8. **What went wrong in 1.18 development was mostly fragmentation, not monotony.** The snapshot notes keep fixing "microbiomes", temperature clashes, "flat areas a bit too flat and featureless", and rivers ending in dry gorges. The fix for rivers was to "carve a fjord… or raise the terrain to form a saddle valley".
9. **Mojang's method was tools plus fast reloading.** They built map overlays of noise values with highlight filters, in-game density and side-profile views, toggles for each cave system, and live chunk reloads (Dev Q&A). Vintage Story ships `/wgen regen` for the same reason. Our `--map` and `--shot` loop is the same idea.
10. **Caves take three cooperating systems.** Noise caves at three scales, an entrance noise biased toward Y −10…30, and aquifers that decide whether each cell is flooded, dry or lava, with stone barriers between cells. Without aquifers every cave below sea level fills with water (Dev Q&A).

## 1. Vanilla terrain (1.18+)

### Multi-noise biome space and splines
- **(a) For the player:** big, coherent landforms (inland mountains, coasts, flat eroded plains) that any climate can sit on. You get deserts on hills and snowy plateaus. Mojang: "the shape of the terrain is no longer always be determined by the biome… biomes are now simply layered on top of the terrain" (https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/caves---cliffs-part-ii-the-features).
- **(b) How:** Six parameters: temperature (5 bands), humidity (5 bands), continentalness (7 bands from mushroom fields through deep ocean to far inland), erosion (7 bands; higher means flatter), weirdness folded into peaks-and-valleys `PV = 1−|3|w|−2|` (5 bands), and depth (about 0 at the surface, rising 1/128 per block downward). A biome is the entry whose interval box is closest in this 6D space. Only continentalness, erosion and PV feed splines that output the terrain **offset** (height), **factor** (vertical stretch) and **jaggedness** (https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation). In the data, `depth = gradient(y −64→1.5, 320→−1.5) + offset` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/depth.json), and `offset` is `−0.50375 + spline(...)`.
- **(c) inference:** Our simulated heightfield already plays the part of offset. Continentalness, erosion and PV are proxies for distance to sea, flatness and ridge versus valley, and we can measure all of these directly from the simulated grid: distance to coast, slope or relief, drainage area and curvature. Cost: a few derived 512² fields, close to free. Do not copy the noises.

### 3D density, overhangs, shattered terrain
- **(a)** Overhangs, small arches and "shattered" spiky ground. The snapshot notes say these keep flat areas from being "a bit too flat and featureless" (https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/new-world-generation-java-available-testing).
- **(b)** `sloped_cheese = 4·quarter_negative((depth + jaggedness·half_negative(jagged noise))·factor) + base_3d_noise` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/sloped_cheese.json). A block is solid where `final_density > 0` (https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router). The 3D noise is "old blended noise" with xz_factor 80 and y_factor 160 (`base_3d_noise.json`). Because the noise varies in y, the 0 crossing can fold back on itself, and that is what makes overhangs. A large `factor` squashes the effect, and a small one lets it dominate, which is how shattered terrain appears. The final density is computed on 4×8×4 cells and interpolated (`final_density.json`: `cell_size` 4/8/4).
- **(c) inference:** This fits in our 256 m voxel volume as `density = (surface_height − y)/scale + A(x,z)·noise3d`, where A is a designed amplitude field chosen from simulated context (cliff faces, hard rock, coastal erosion), and zero elsewhere. Cost: one 3D noise per voxel near the player. The risk is that distant height columns won't show the overhangs, which is fine because they are small.

### Noise caves, entrances, carvers, aquifers
- **(a)** Big chambers (cheese), long tunnels (spaghetti), thin cracks (noodle, "1 to 5 blocks"), pillars inside chambers, natural-looking mouths, and underground lakes at their own levels (https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cave).
- **(b)** `final_density` mixes them by range: where `sloped_cheese < 1.5625` (near the surface) it takes `min(sloped_cheese, 5·entrances)`. Deeper down it combines cheese (`cave_cheese` and `cave_layer` noises), 2D spaghetti, roughness and entrances, and adds pillars back. Noodles are applied by a final `min` (`final_density.json`, grep of component names). Entrances use `cave_entrance` noise + 0.37 + a gradient from 0.3 at Y=−10 to 0 at Y=30, combined with two `abs(spaghetti_3d)` tunnel fields whose scale is picked by a rarity noise (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/density_function/overworld/caves/entrances.json). Carvers still run afterwards. Ordinary caves go from Y −56 to 180 and have a 1-in-4 chance of a 5–15 block main room. Canyons (ravines) start between Y 10 and 72 (https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cave, https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation). Aquifers split space into 16×12×16 cells, each with a jittered centre, and each cell is flooded, dry or holds its own fluid level. Stone barriers form between neighbours that differ. Lava appears only at or below Y −10 (https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cave). Mojang on why: "by default all caves below y63 are water filled if we generate caves using the new noise approach… So we had to figure out a way to enable caves below y63 to be dry sometimes" (https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/caves---cliffs-update--part-ii-dev-q-a).
- **(c) inference:** Caves fit only inside our 256 m voxel volume, which is enough since nobody sees caves from far away. Cave mouths are the walking-scale payoff. Placing them by cause would mean putting entrances where the simulation says the rock is soluble or the slope is steep, near valley walls, rather than everywhere. Aquifers are directly relevant to `water.odin`: a cave needs either a local water level or a guaranteed dry state, or our water will flood it. Cost: moderate. Three 3D noises per voxel plus a cell-level fluid rule.

### Cave biomes
- **(b)** They are picked through the depth parameter. Cave biomes use depth 0.2–0.9. Deep dark uses depth 1.1 and erosion −1…−0.375, which is mountain interiors. Dripstone sits under high continentalness, far inland. Lush caves sit under high humidity (https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation, https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cave). The current data has 5 cave biomes: `deep_dark`, `dripstone_caves`, `lush_caves`, `sulfur_caves`, `ice_caves` (https://api.github.com/repos/misode/mcmeta/contents/data/minecraft/worldgen/biome?ref=data). The last two are newer than my training and may be unreleased.
- **(c) inference:** "Under wet forest gives lush caves, under dry far-inland ground gives dripstone" is a causal rule we could take whole, using our wetness and distance-to-coast fields.

### Surface rules and ore veins
Surface rules run after the base terrain. They pick the block from the biome, noise thresholds and depth or water conditions. Deepslate replaces stone below Y 0 with a gradient up to Y 8. Ore veins (copper or iron) come from three noises: toggle, ridged and gap (https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation, https://minecraft.wiki/w/Noise_router).

## 2. Vanilla inventory, with numbers

From the current data (`biome` directory listing above, and https://api.github.com/repos/misode/mcmeta/contents/data/minecraft/worldgen/placed_feature?ref=data):

- **Biomes:** 68 files. Taking out 5 Nether, 5 End and `the_void` leaves **57 overworld biomes: 52 surface and 5 cave**. The wiki counts 52 + 4 for released versions (https://minecraft.wiki/w/Biome).
- **Placed features:** **299** across all dimensions. **Structures:** 52 (`worldgen/structure`).
- **Trees:** the wiki gives 10 species and 19 base shapes: oak 3, spruce/pine 4, birch 2, jungle 4, acacia, dark oak, azalea, cherry, pale oak, mangrove 2 (https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tree). It also has 6 fallen-tree features (`fallen_oak/birch/super_birch/spruce/jungle/poplar_tree`), leaf-litter variants of the main trees, and bee-nest variants.
- **Ground plants:** the placed-feature names include about 50 `patch_*` features. These cover grass for each climate (normal, plain, forest, taiga, jungle, savanna, badlands, meadow), tall grass, large fern, dead bush, dry grass, berry bush, cactus, sugar cane, pumpkin, melon, waterlily, sunflower, firefly bush near water, bush, and leaf litter. There are also about 11 `flower_*` lists, `wildflowers_*`, and brown and red mushrooms in 5 variants each.
- **Small terrain decorations:** `forest_rock` (mossy-cobble boulders), `disk_sand`, `disk_clay`, `disk_gravel`, `disk_grass`, `spring_water` and `spring_lava`, `lake_lava_surface`, `ice_spike`, `iceberg_*`, `blue_ice`, `desert_well`, `fossil_upper`, `fossil_lower`, `amethyst_geode`, `pointed_dripstone`, `large_dripstone`, `glow_lichen`, `freeze_top_layer` and `snow_layers`. Water lakes were removed in 1.18 and replaced by aquifers (https://minecraft.wiki/w/Feature).
- **Per biome** (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/misode/mcmeta/data/data/minecraft/worldgen/biome/plains.json and siblings): `plains` has 48 features, `forest` 48, `old_growth_spruce_taiga` 52 and `swamp` 52. About 37 are shared: geodes, dungeons, 25 ore or stone blobs (granite, diorite, andesite, tuff, dirt and gravel blobs), disks and springs. What makes each one different is the remaining 10–15:
  - plains: `trees_plains`, `flower_plains`, `patch_grass_plain`, `patch_tall_grass_2`, `patch_bush`, `patch_pumpkin`, `patch_sugar_cane`, `patch_firefly_bush_near_water`
  - forest: `forest_flowers`, `trees_birch_and_oak_leaf_litter`, `patch_grass_forest`, `patch_bush`
  - old growth spruce taiga: `forest_rock`, `patch_large_fern`, `patch_grass_taiga`, `patch_dead_bush`, `brown_mushroom_old_growth`, `red_mushroom_old_growth`, `patch_berry_common`
  - swamp: `trees_swamp`, `patch_waterlily`, `seagrass_swamp`, `patch_firefly_bush_swamp`, `fossil_*`, `disk_clay` only
- **Surface block types:** grass, dirt, coarse dirt, podzol, mycelium, mud, sand, red sand, sandstone, gravel, terracotta bands, snow, powder snow, packed ice, calcite and stone (https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation, https://minecraft.wiki/w/Biome).
- **(c) inference:** At our scale, three to five decorations per habitat would cover the gap: boulders, fallen logs with mushrooms, leaf litter, ferns, berry bushes, and something near water such as reeds or fireflies. Each needs a placement rule fed by our slope, wetness and elevation fields. Cost: small. Each is a few lines plus some blocks, and there is no new system.

## 3. Terrain mods used as visual reference

### ReTerraForged (source: https://github.com/racoonman2/ReTerraForged, branch 1.20.2)
- **Terrain types:** `TerrainType.java` registers steppe, plains, hills_1/2, dales, plateau, badlands, torridonian, mountains_1/2/3, dolomites, mountains_cliffs, mountain_chain, volcano and volcano_pipe, plus water and island types: river, river_banks, lake, wetland, lagoon, archipelago and more (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/racoonman2/ReTerraForged/1.20.2/common/src/main/java/raccoonman/reterraforged/world/worldgen/terrain/TerrainType.java).
- **Blending by region:** `TerrainProvider.generateTerrain` takes 8 mixable populators (steppe, plains, dales, hills ×2, torridonian, plateau, badlands) and adds every pairwise blend. Each blend uses a domain-warped Perlin selector at half the region scale and is tagged as a composite type. Mountains ×3, badlands and volcano stay unblended. The list is shuffled, and a weighted `RegionSelector` gives each cell's region ID one populator (`TerrainProvider.java`, `terrain/region/RegionSelector.java`). The region size is the `terrainRegionSize` setting.
- **Erosion filter:** `tile/filter/Erosion.java` is particle (droplet) hydraulic erosion. Droplets are seeded per chunk on each iteration and step down the gradient with 0.05/0.95 inertia. Carrying capacity is `max(−Δh·speed·water·4, 0.01)`. Erosion is spread with a radius-4 brush, deposit is bilinear, and water evaporates at 0.99 per step. The settings are `dropletsPerChunk`, `dropletLifetime`, `dropletVolume`, `dropletVelocity`, `erosionRate` and `depositeRate`, plus a separate smoothing pass (`FilterSettings.java`). This looks to me like the widely copied Sebastian Lague droplet erosion (inference).
- **Surface by slope:** default `SurfaceSettings.Erosion(rockVariance 30, rockMin 140, dirtVariance 40, dirtMin 95, snowHeight 95, rockSteepness 0.65, dirtSteepness 0.475, screeSteepness 0.4, snowSteepness 0.45, heightModifier 6, slopeModifier 3)` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/racoonman2/ReTerraForged/1.20.2/common/src/main/java/raccoonman/reterraforged/data/preset/settings/Preset.java). In practice there are graded bands: scree, then dirt, then exposed rock as slope rises, and rock or snow above heights that are jittered by noise.
- **Rivers and lakes:** there are `rivermap/` classes (`RiverGenerator`, `Network`, `RiverWarp`, `LakeConfig`, `WetlandConfig`) and a `WaterfallPopulator`. I did not read them, so I can't say how the network is built.
- **(c) inference:** The steepness bands transfer directly and cheaply, and they are an upgrade on our single "steep means stone" rule: scree and gravel at the toe of steep slopes, dirt in between, rock on the face, with noise on each threshold. The region-of-designed-types idea already exists in our simulation as rock erodibility zones. Use the zone label to pick a designed micro-shape (terraces in torridonian sandstone, tors on granite) instead of blending noise generators. We already simulate erosion, so the droplet filter adds nothing for us.

### Terralith (https://github.com/Stardust-Labs-MC/Terralith, branch 1.20, last pushed 2025-02; may lag the current release)
- "Over 95 brand new biomes", canyons, shattered biomes, floating islands, deep ocean trenches, Yosemite Cliffs, skylands, and cave biomes such as Underground Jungle, Infested, Fungal and Frostfire caves (https://modrinth.com/datapack/terralith).
- **Cliffs, arches and dunes from density functions only:** the repo has 294 biome files and density-function groups `cliff/` (`carve`, `carve_depth`, `cliff_depth`, `max_cut`, `slope`, `spline`, `modified_offset`), `arch/` (`base`, `height_spline`, `thickness`, `total`), `spikes/` (`spike_x`, `spike_z`, `tendrils`), `dune/`, and `special/` (calderas, craters). Its `cliff/carve` adds a cliff spline to `max(−1.65 + max_cut noise, range_choice(...))`: a flat noise caps the terrain and so cuts a terrace step. `arch/total` uses `range_choice` on a height spline, so the arch is a solid band of designed thickness hanging at a designed height. It also overrides vanilla `offset`, `factor`, `jaggedness` and `sloped_cheese` (repo tree; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Stardust-Labs-MC/Terralith/1.20/data/terralith/worldgen/density_function/overworld/cliff/carve.json).
- **(c) inference:** These are designed landform SDFs added to the density in our voxel zone. The lesson is that each landform is its own small function with its own mask. Masking them by cause (arches in coastal hard rock, cliffs where hard rock meets a river cut) is our addition. Cost: small per landform. One catch: our distant height columns must see the 2D part of the shape, the step height, or the landform will pop in as the player approaches.

### Tectonic (https://modrinth.com/mod/tectonic)
- Continent-like landmasses "thousands to tens of thousands of blocks wide", ranges "tens of thousands of blocks" long, deeper oceans, underground rivers that carry rivers through mountains too tall for them and rejoin surface rivers, deep lava tunnels, jungle pillars "upwards of a hundred blocks tall", very large badlands canyons, desert dunes, 2–3 tier plateaus, and lowland wetlands with frequent ponds and lakes. It works with Terrablender and Biolith.
- **Unverified:** how it is built. The GitHub README (https://github.com/Apollounknowndev/tectonic) doesn't describe it, and I did not read the source.

## 4. Realism-first block games

### TerraFirmaCraft (source: https://github.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft, branch 1.21.x)
- **Rocks:** 20 types. Sedimentary 7 (shale, claystone, limestone, conglomerate, dolomite, chert, chalk), metamorphic 6 (quartzite, slate, phyllite, schist, gneiss, marble), igneous extrusive 4 (rhyolite, basalt, andesite, dacite) and intrusive 3 (granite, diorite, gabbro). A column usually has "two or three different rock layers". Regions "can be over a kilometer across". Uplift exposes deep rock in mountains, and rare dikes push intrusive rock up through the layers above (https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Field-Guide/en_us/the_world/geology.html).
- **Region pipeline** (`RegionGenerator.java`, task list): init, then distance to cell edge, boundary types (converging or diverging plates), continents and ocean depths, flood-fill small oceans, hotspots, distance to ocean and deep ocean, base land height, distance to west coast, mountains and barrier islands, islands, biome altitude, climate, rocks, karst surface, rivers and lakes, and finally **choose biomes**. Cells are about 12 km (code comment) (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/region/RegionGenerator.java).
- **125 landform "biomes"**, each designed for a cause: drumlins, knob_and_kettle, patterned_ground, stone_circles, tuyas, hoodoos, buttes, mesas, stair_step_canyons, whorled_canyons, setback_cliffs, sea_stacks, salt_flats, tidal_flats, rift_valley and rift_lake, four shield-volcano ages, glaciated, glacially carved and ice-sheet mountain variants, and karst in four styles (cenote, doline, shilin, tower) at several altitudes (repo tree, `data/tfc/worldgen/biome`).
- **Rules** (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/1.21.x/src/main/java/net/dries007/tfc/world/region/ChooseBiomes.java): islands pick from a list. A point near a cell edge with positive divergence becomes a rift valley or lake. Rivers become `RIVER_VALLEY` unless they cross collision mountains, rifts or ice. Mountains on converging edges are chosen by temperature against `−16 + 0.006·rainfall`: ice-sheet, then glaciated, then glacially carved, then plain. Otherwise there are separate low/mid/high altitude tables for normal, desert, semi-arid, paleo-ice-sheet and ice-sheet climates. Karst surface rock with rain over 375 becomes karst, and with rain over 425 and `rain + 10·temp > 500` becomes tower karst. Patterned ground with rain under 145 becomes stone circles.
- **Trees:** 20 woods: acacia, ash, aspen, birch, blackwood, chestnut, douglas fir, hickory, kapok, mangrove, maple, oak, palm, pine, rosewood, sequoia, spruce, sycamore, white cedar, willow (`common/blocks/wood/Wood.java`). There are 101 configured tree features. Oak, for example, picks randomly among 8 NBT templates and also has large and dead forms (`worldgen/configured_feature/tree/oak.json`). Each `*_entry` gives a climate window and the forest floor. Oak: temperature −0.4…17.6, groundwater 210…500, rain variance −0.5…0.75. Groundcover is oak twigs and fallen leaves, with a dead tree, fallen log, bush log and leaves, and an `alfisol_disc` soil patch (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TerraFirmaCraft/TerraFirmaCraft/1.21.x/src/main/resources/data/tfc/worldgen/configured_feature/tree/oak_entry.json).
- **Plants and soils:** `Plant.java` lists about 158 species entries. These are my count of enum names before the shape types and include shoreline animals such as barnacles. They come in 10 land categories (standard, dry, cactus, creeping, epiphyte, short and tall grass, vines, weeping, twisting) and 6 water categories. Appearance changes with season (https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Field-Guide/en_us/the_world/flora.html). There are 29 wild crops, each with a temperature and rainfall window (https://terrafirmacraft.github.io/Field-Guide/en_us/the_world/wild_crops.html). Soils in 1.21.x are 8 soil orders: entisol, aridisol, oxisol, fluvisol, andisol, podzol, alfisol, mollisol (`SoilBlockType.java`). Older versions had 4 textures (silt, loam and so on). That is from memory and unverified.
- **(c) inference:** `ChooseBiomes` is almost exactly our principle written as code, and it can be applied after the fact. Once our simulation has finished, label each region by measured cause (rock hardness, drainage, slope, coast distance, a climate proxy) and stamp a designed micro-landform on the heightfield where the label fits. Cost: a short rules table, plus one small shape function per landform. The forest-entry pattern (climate window, groundcover, fallen log, soil disc) is the cheapest walking-scale win we found anywhere.

### Vintage Story
- **Pipeline:** 7 noise maps (climate, flower, bush, forest, beach, geologic province, landform), then landforms, rock strata, caves, block layers, deposits, structures, ponds, vegetation and patches, and a finalise pass (https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Terrain_Generation).
- **Landforms JSON:** `terrainOctaves` (10 amplitudes, always 10), `terrainOctaveThresholds` (which can make an octave appear only some of the time, for example a 0.7 threshold gives bumps about 30 % of the time), and `terrainYKeyPositions` with `terrainYKeyThresholds`, a density profile over height with sea level at 0.43. "A sharp change in position with little change in density creates cliffs." A narrow density change at sea level makes flat beaches. There is also a `weight` and `useClimateMap`, and `/wgen regen 5` reloads `landforms.json` live (https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:WorldGen_Configuration).
- **Rock strata:** sedimentary on top (optional), then metamorphic (optional), then igneous (always). Basalt can appear at any layer, halite forms domes, and suevite appears only in craters (Terrain Generation page). The Rock page names 22 rocks but also says "Twenty types". There are six province types: Shield, Platform, Orogen, Basin, Large Igneous Province, Extended Crust. There are usually 3 layers, or 4 when volcanic (https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Rock).
- **Trees:** the Trees page lists 16 types with worldgen windows for temperature, rain, fertility, forestation and elevation. For example, oak is 2–22 °C, rain 95–170, elevation 0–0.7, and larch is −17 to −3 °C. There are sub-variants: dead, swamp, Scots and fir pine, Himalayan birch and others (https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Trees). The tree generator is recursive. Each trunk has a width that shrinks by `widthLoss` per block and dies at `dieAt`. Angles evolve with `angleVertEvolve` and are pulled down by `gravityDrag`. Children spawn at `branchStart`, `branchSpacing` and `branchQuantity`, scaled by `branchWidthMultiplier`. Each nesting level reads the next entry of the `branches` array. Worldgen can add moss, vines and resin logs (https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:Trees).
- **(c) inference:** The y-key density profile is a 1D designed curve. In our voxel zone, a per-landform profile could give stepped cliffs or flat-topped mesas without noise. The branch grower is a good fit for "more tree variety in small code": one grower, about 10 parameters, with each species as a data row picked by our wetness, elevation and exposure fields. Cost: roughly 150–250 lines for the grower, then data. I did not find how VS overhangs arise. My reading is that its 3D noise compared against the y-profile allows them.

## 5. What players and developers say went right or wrong

- **Pre-1.18 problem:** terrain shape was decided by biome. 1.18 decoupled them so "biomes are now simply layered on top of the terrain" (Mojang features article, above). The Dev Q&A says "The worldgen changes in 1.18 were so big that we mostly had to recreate terrain generation from scratch" (https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/caves---cliffs-update--part-ii-dev-q-a).
- **Snapshot fixes** (experimental worldgen notes, https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/new-world-generation-java-available-testing):
  - reduce "cold microbiomes, such as a small splotch of snowy tundra in the middle of a forest (birdpoop microbiomes)"
  - later "allow for more noisiness and diversity again… microbiomes… will usually be of matching temperatures"
  - small hills and overhangs re-added because flat areas were "a bit too flat and featureless"
  - rivers in mountains now "carve a fjord… or raise the terrain to form a saddle valley… friendlier for both walking and boating"
  - peaks above about y260 are "mercilessly cut off into a plateau" to leave building room
  - Stony Peaks added "to avoid temperature clashes such as a snowcapped peak sticking up from a jungle"
  - water springs allowed in dirt and snow "increasing the likelihood of small mountain streams and waterfalls"
- **Process:** "a tool that shows a map of the world with various overlays such as terrain offset, multinoise values, biomes etc. And filters to highlight things like specific noise ranges… debug overlays… side-profile view… toggle things on and off… reload chunks dynamically" (Dev Q&A). Hang Son Doong in Vietnam was the reference for cheese caves (same source).
- **Player feedback on the snapshots** pulled both ways. Some found biomes "too frequent, too clustered, and too small" and "only one chunk big". Others asked for "more flatlands between mountains" and said biomes "may get boring over time". One suggested that microbiomes occur only in predefined combinations (https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/community/posts/4404932443405/comments/4404970583949 and sibling comments; secondary summary through search, and I did not read every comment).
- **(c) inference for us:** two lessons. Variety must come in *coherent* patches; random noise salt reads as error. And walkability (valleys that connect, saddles, no dead-end gorges) mattered to Mojang as much as spectacle.
