# What large animals live on the reference islands

Written 2026-11-12 for step 12, trails, which the plan had laid by deer before the plants were changed to those of the warm islands of southern Japan. The question: which large animal makes trails on islands like the eight the land is measured against (`real-islands.md`), and on which of them. Read from search summaries of the pages linked; none of the papers was opened. Where a statement is from memory it says so.

## What the sources say

- **Tsushima** has a deer of its own, the Tsushima sika deer, and many of them. A 1997 study of its evergreen broadleaf forest chose four areas by deer density: about 74.9, 22.8 and 3.6 deer a km², and one with none, with fewer plant species the more deer ([Biosphere Conservation 4(1)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/browse/biospherecons/4/1/_contents); the island's mice fall as deer rise, [5(2)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/biospherecons/5/2/5_KJ00003137032/_article/-char/en)). Wild boar were killed out on Tsushima long ago and are back from animals brought in some 30 years since (a city interview on a hunting blog, [inohoi](https://inohoi.jp/blogs/knowledge/210118); not checked against an official record).
- **The Izu islands** have no large wild animal of their own. Mikurajima has no native mammal but bats; its others are feral cats and two rats ([Mammal Research 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13364-020-00544-5)). What deer and goats there are, people brought: sika deer on Niijima came from seventeen let loose on the islet of Jinai in 1969 and 1971 for visitors ([PMC 7131996](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7131996)); the muntjac of Izu Ōshima escaped from a park and came to outnumber the people ([ABS-CBN 2023](https://www.abs-cbn.com/classified-odd/11/24/23/barking-deer-in-japan-causing-havoc)); 1,137 feral goats were removed from Hachijōjima from 2001 to 2007 (search summary of [NIES](https://www.nies.go.jp/biodiversity/invasive/DB/etoc1_mammals.html)). No record was found of deer, boar or goats on Toshima, Kōzushima or Aogashima (Aogashima has cattle).
- **Ogasawara** (Hahajima, Chichijima) has no native land mammal but a fruit bat (from memory). Goats that people left there in the 19th century (from memory) stripped whole islands: a third of Nakōdojima was bare red soil and rock by 1997, and 940 goats were taken off Mukojima from 1998 ([Japan Times, June 1997](https://japantimes.co.jp/news/1997/06/23/national/wild-goat-herds-face-culling-to-save-ogasawaras), [July 1997](https://japantimes.co.jp/news/1997/07/16/national/ogasawaras-ready-to-get-their-goats)). They have been removed from every uninhabited island and now live only on Chichijima ([Ministry of the Environment](https://www.env.go.jp/nature/isan/worldheritage/en/ogasawara/measure/index.html), [Tokyo](https://www.kankyo1.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/naturepark/english/know/park/ogasawara_isan/vegetation.html)); Ototojima also had feral pigs ([action plan](https://ogasawara-info.jp/pdf/science/h21_04_shiryou3_4.pdf)).
- **Kikaijima**: nothing found. The Ryukyu wild boar is the largest mammal of Amami Ōshima, 25 km away ([MLIT](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R4-00468.html)), but no source listed it on Kikaijima, which a Kagoshima museum report calls small and little wooded. Raised coral, it may never have been reached; unverified.
- **Deer and boar swim.** Sika deer cross between the Tomogashima islets and the mainland both ways ([Mammal Study 45(2)](https://bioone.org/journals/mammal-study/volume-45/issue-2/ms2019-0048/Bi-Directional-Movement-of-Deer-between-Tomogashima-Islands-and-the/10.3106/ms2019-0048.full)). So an island near larger land, or joined to it when the sea stood low, has them; one far out in the ocean has none unless people brought them.

## How many trails

No measured length of deer trail a km² was found. The nearest figures: lidar mapped 2,829 km of trails and tracks of every kind, animal and human, over 59 km² of Canadian boreal forest, up to 68 km a km² in peatland ([McDermid 2025](https://www.cclmportal.ca/sites/default/files/2025-06/McDermid%202025%20Mapping%20Trails%20and%20Tracks%20in%20the%20Boreal%20LiDAR%20CNN.pdf)); people's roads, trails and cutlines run 0.1 to 3.3 km a km² by watershed in the Rockies ([Y2Y 2022](https://y2y.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Mapping-recreational.12.2022.v3.pdf)). The streams on these islands run 0.9 to 1.6 km a km² (`real-islands.md`).

## Step 28: who reached which island

Added 2026-11-15, when the islands gained neighbours and a continent. Read from search summaries; no paper was opened.

- **How far deer swim.** By a search summary of a study of the deer of Okinoshima (Tomogashima), not opened, sika deer can swim up to 12 km (citing Feldhamer 1980), have been seen swimming round the island, and Awaji's deer are 4 km from it; the study of deer moving between the Tomogashima islets and the Kii peninsula is [Mammal Study 45(2)](https://bioone.org/journals/mammal-study/volume-45/issue-2/ms2019-0048/Bi-Directional-Movement-of-Deer-between-Tomogashima-Islands-and-the/10.3106/ms2019-0048.full). A sika deer met at sea off Vladivostok in 2015 had most likely swum about 12 km across the bay ([Moscow Times](https://themoscowtimes.com/2015/07/06/russian-fishermen-come-across-swimming-deer-at-sea-video-a47916)). Kinkazan's deer are seen swimming its 0.6 km strait (search summary).
- **Ayu on islands.** Ayu were found on seven of eight islands in four groups of the Tsushima current (Cheju, Gotō, Tsushima, Oki), their differences put down to drift on small islands over several thousand years ([Japanese Journal of Ichthyology 45(2)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jji1950/45/2/45_2_87/_article/-char/en)). On Amami Ōshima the young of the Ryukyu ayu grow up in brackish water rather than at sea (Kishino and Shinomiya 2005, search summary); on Yakushima they spend about four months in estuary and sea (Hirano et al. 2022). That the Tsushima current's islands were joined to larger land when the sea stood 120 m lower, and that no Izu island has ayu, is from memory.
- **Skinks reached the Izu islands across the sea**: Okada's five-lined skink crossed from the mainland to the proto-islands some 3 to 7.6 million years ago, perhaps more than once ([Dryad, Brandley et al.](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.v47s1)). Not built: nothing draws a skink.
