# What birds and fish live at the cliffs and pools of the reference islands

Written 2026-11-12 for step 16, birds at cliffs and fish in pools. The question: which seabirds, cliff birds and fish of streams and rock pools truly live on islands like ours (Yakushima, Tanegashima, the Tokara and Izu islands, about 30° N), where on the island, when, and how each moves; and which an island of ours can honestly host. Read from search summaries and the abstracts of the pages linked; no paper was read in full. Where a statement is from memory it says so.

## Birds

- **Streaked shearwater** (*Calonectris leucomelas*, オオミズナギドリ). Mikurajima is called the world's largest breeding ground; the birds hunt fish at sea by day and come back to the forest at night, where they burrow in the floor of the evergreen *Castanopsis* forest and climb the trunks to take off ([Tokyo Metropolitan Government](https://www.kankyo1.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/naturepark/english/know/park/introduction/kokuritsu/fujihakone/mikurashima/animals_and_plants.html)). One colony on Mikurajima held about 6,600 burrows in 3.4 ha, densest (over 0.5 a m²) along the cliff edge ([Yamashina Institute, 1985](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jyio1952/19/1/19_1_56/_article/-char/en)). The colony has fallen fast in 40 years, and cats killed six of eight birds whose carcasses were tested ([Mammal Study 47(3)](https://bioone.org/journals/mammal-study/volume-47/issue-3/ms2021-0047/Cats-were-Responsible-for-the-Headless-Carcasses-of-Shearwaters/10.3106/ms2021-0047.full)). It also breeds on the Kusagaki islets west of Kagoshima ([WBSJ, IBA 156](https://wbsj.org/nature/hogo/others/iba/eng/156.html)). Shearwaters fly by dynamic soaring, across the wind, climbing into it and falling away ([study of streaked shearwaters in storms](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/191689/8/Lempidakis_et_al_Seabirds_fly_into_the_eye_of_the_storm_-_Main_text__Resubmission__4__No_tracked_changes_.pdf)). That it winters in the tropics and is away from about November to March is from memory.
- **Black kite** (*Milvus migrans lineatus*, トビ). Breeds on the Kusagaki islets ([IBA 156](https://wbsj.org/nature/hogo/others/iba/eng/156.html)); common throughout Japan except the Ryukyus ([Kyushu University](https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/23900/p247.pdf)); lives mostly on coasts, soaring on updrafts with little flapping to scan for food below ([orbis-pictus](https://orbis-pictus.jp/en/article/tobi.php)); seen soaring over the cliffs and stacks of the Jōgasaki coast in Izu ([same](https://orbis-pictus.jp/en/articles/tobi.php)). A span of about 1.5 m and a forked tail are from memory.
- **Osprey** (*Pandion haliaetus*, ミサゴ). From Hokkaido to Kyushu, near-threatened ([Japan Times](https://japantimes.co.jp/life/2007/11/28/environment/osprey)), and the Izu islands ([Hyōgo red data](https://www.kankyo.pref.hyogo.lg.jp/download_file/view/23254/22177)); a breeding resident of the Kusagaki islets ([IBA 156](https://wbsj.org/nature/hogo/others/iba/eng/156.html)); watched breeding on Mageshima, 7 km west of Tanegashima (a Ministry of Defence survey, [Kyushu](https://www.mod.go.jp/rdb/kyushu/contract/construction/kumamoto/jyunbisyo/pdf/jyunbisyo_136.pdf)). It nests on big trees or on rocks and cliff tops by coasts and river mouths ([Ōita red data](https://www.pref.oita.jp/10550/reddata2011/pdf/rdb/05/ch019.pdf)); in Hokkaido more than half of 62 nests were on rocks or cliffs, which the authors call uncommon elsewhere in the world ([Condor 113(4)](https://bioone.org/journals/The-Condor/volume-113/issue-4/cond.2011.110041/The-Status-and-Breeding-Biology-of-Ospreys-in-Hokkaido-Japan/10.1525/cond.2011.110041.full)). That it hovers on beating wings before it dives is from memory.
- **Peregrine falcon** (ハヤブサ). Resident and nesting on sea cliffs throughout Japan, the Izu islands among its range; it lays on a bare ledge, from March ([Bird Research](https://www.bird-research.jp/1/seitai/5_12_peregrinus.pdf), [Ministry of the Environment](https://ikilog.biodic.go.jp/rdbdata/files/envpdf/%E9%B3%A5%E9%A1%9E_081.pdf)); a breeding resident of the Kusagaki islets. Few pairs: Ōita has under ten breeding birds ([Ōita](https://www.pref.oita.jp/10550/reddata/data/text/314.pdf)).
- **Pacific reef heron** (*Egretta sacra*, クロサギ). Lives on rocky coasts; dark grey north of Kyushu, white more often to the south ([orbis-pictus](https://orbis-pictus.jp/en/articles/kurosagi.php), [Condor 93(2)](https://sora-dev.unm.edu/sites/default/files/journals/condor/v093n02/p0383-p0389.pdf)); it stalks the wave-washed rock alone, crouching when a wave comes ([Strix 42](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/strix/42/0/42_23/_article/-char/en)).
- **Black-tailed gull** (ウミネコ). Nests from Hokkaido to western Kyushu ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-tailed_gull)); its great colonies are in the north (Kabushima, Teuri). No colony was found at 30° N.
- **Brown booby** (カツオドリ). Breeds on the Kusagaki islets, about 50 ha in all ([IBA 156](https://wbsj.org/nature/hogo/others/iba/eng/156.html)), and on islets of the Ryukyus and Ogasawara (388 nests on one Mukojima islet in 2023, [Monitoring Sites 1000](https://www.biodic.go.jp/moni1000/findings/reports/pdf/2023_seabirds.pdf)): small remote islets, not the high islands.

## Fish

- **Falls cut off the fish.** At 55 sites on Yakushima's streams, 31 below any fall and 24 above one, Kano et al. found eleven diadromous species and one brought in (masu salmon), and no fish at all at 18 of the sites above falls; a fall of more than 5 m below a site had a significant effect on what lived there (Ichthyological Research 61: 305–316, 2014; its abstract was read through [an index](https://opact.sdu.edu.tr/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c512t-9e405a0ce043877244ef80a76eefed5125d76ab473f4ca2c448d0ea4dba08fb83) and [researchmap](https://researchmap.jp/midoriiida/published_papers/12483545?lang=ja)). The one exception was the goby *Rhinogobius brunneus* (yoshinobori), living above falls of 50 m (Nunobiki) and 88 m (Ōko); its otoliths showed it never went to sea there, though its genes were not cut off from the fish below. That the other six of the 24 sites held these gobies is an inference.
- **An eDNA study of twelve Yakushima rivers** found fewer diadromous species in steeper rivers with smaller estuaries, and assumed 17 diadromous species for the island ([Marine and Freshwater Research, MF24029](https://publish.csiro.au/mf/mf24029)).
- **Ayu** (*Plecoglossus altivelis*, 鮎). Recorded in five Yakushima rivers (Nagata, Issō, Miyanoura, Anbō, Kurio), at the southern limit of its range, at low density, and gone from the Anbō; it spawns in the Kurio in mid-November, the larvae spend about four months at sea, and the young come up the rivers in early April ([Hirano et al. 2022](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jji/69/2/69_22-002/_article/-char/en)). Elsewhere, hatching about two weeks after autumn spawning in the lower river, and the larvae drift to the sea ([Shimanto estuary](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/647244ac53aa8c89630423a8)). That it grazes algae from the stones of riffles and runs, holds facing the current, grows to about 20 cm by late summer and dies after spawning is from memory.
- **Bōzuhaze** (*Sicyopterus japonicus*). A goby that grazes algae on the stones of the middle and upper reaches and climbs falls with its mouth and its fins as suckers ([Churaumi](https://churaumi.okinawa/en/fishbook/00000465), [Japanese Journal of Ichthyology 26](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jji1950/26/1/26_1_84/_article)).
- **Eels** were found above a 46 m fall, which they must have climbed; nobody has seen them do it ([news summary](https://mail.stofficetokyo.ch/news/environment/how-eels-best-a-tall-waterfall-may-offer-a-clue-for-their-survival); which river was not given). That eels hide under stones by day and feed at night is from memory.
- **Rock pools.** In a tide-pool community of south-western Japan the dusky frillgoby (*Bathygobius fuscus*) was active by day and blennies (*Istiblennius*) more often by night ([Ichthyological Research](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10228-013-0351-1)). Blennies and gobies make up most of such communities in the western Pacific ([core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/201065937.pdf)). Every source is about pools the falling tide leaves.

## What that decided

| Kind | Built? | Host on our islands | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black kite | Yes | The three tallest faces (of the place finder's) the wind meets at 1 m/s or more (`lift`), 500 m apart, 2 to 5 birds each, by day | A resident of the islets nearest our latitude, a soarer on the lift of coastal slopes. Every kind has such faces, so every island has kites; at night they roost in the woods and are not drawn |
| Osprey | Yes | The tallest sea stack 8 m or more high: a pair, one on the nest on its top, one circling over the sea off it and hanging on beating wings | Nests on rocks and cliff tops round Japan, more than elsewhere; a stack is out of reach of anything that climbs. Every kind has stacks, so every island has a pair |
| Ayu | Yes | Streams of 0.05 m³/s or more with no fall over 2.5 m between them and the sea, from day 90 to 324 (April to November), growing from 8 to 20 cm | Yakushima's five rivers, its southern limit. Fall of 2.5 m: Kano's 5 m was for all fish; ayu leap low weirs and do not climb (from memory). On the cone and crater of seed 3 it is (almost) absent: their streams are small and broken by falls |
| Yoshinobori | Yes | Every stream and lake with no fall over 5 m below, and above such a fall in a quarter of streams, drawn by the fall, where it got up first | Kano's 5 m, and his landlocked yoshinobori above 50 and 88 m falls; the quarter is a guess from 6 of 24 sites |
| Streaked shearwater | No | It would fit every island (forest by a cliff, no cats) | It is at sea by day and comes to land in the dark, so a player would see a raft on the sea at dusk and birds overhead at night: a third motion, a third time of day and a pose on the water. The best next row |
| Peregrine | No | A ledge on the tallest face | One pair, fast and high: a speck. Left for a second cliff bird |
| Pacific reef heron | No | The wave-washed rock (`washed`) | A good row for the platform: one bird standing. Left out to keep this step to two birds |
| Black-tailed gull | No | | Breeds no further south than western Kyushu; the brief's "gull on a stack" is not true at 30° N |
| Brown booby | No | | Breeds on small remote islets, not on high islands like ours |
| Eels, bōzuhaze, flagtails | No | | Eels hide by day; bōzuhaze is a second goby on the stones of riffles, too small to show (see below) |
| Rock-pool fish | No | | Our sea has no tide, so the platform's gullies are open slots of the sea, not pools the tide leaves |

## What the pictures showed

- **Birds can be seen to about 300 m, not 1 km.** A kite's 1.6 m span is about 1.6 pixels at 1 km on a 2560-pixel frame, and fog and the forest behind take that; none showed in a frame from 1 km. They are specks from about 300 m, read as birds against the sky from about 60 m, and as block birds (body, pale head, wings with pale primaries, tail) within about 30 m. Three to five kites at a face are never a flock.
- **Fish at life size do not read.** Seen from a bank 3 to 5 m off, through stream water drawn 62% opaque, a 20 cm ayu is a sliver 6 pixels wide (its true width, an eighth of its length) and a 7 cm goby a speck. They are drawn twice (ayu) and two and a half times (goby) life size, and wider than life (a quarter of their length), with the back's colour on top and the paler flank on the other faces; then they read as small dim shapes holding in the current, which is how fish look from a bank, but not crisply as fish. On the default day (95, early April) the ayu are 8 cm juveniles just up from the sea, so a stream holds mostly gobies until about June.
