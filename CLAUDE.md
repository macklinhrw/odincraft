# OdinCraft v5

A small first-person block game used to test directions for world generation, look and gameplay. The owner decides direction and judges everything visual by looking; record each decision in `docs/open-decisions.md` the moment it is made.

- `docs/ideas.md`: read before proposing a feature.
- `docs/lessons.md`: read before proposing architecture, a plan or a way of working.
- `docs/foundations.md`: the plan for the wider world and the order of work on the island; one design per system in `docs/foundations/`. Read its sections 3 and 8 before adding to the land, rock, water, plants or living things. A plan, not decisions.
- `docs/interesting-world.md`: the research under that plan. Its note `interesting-world/notes/real-islands.md` compares the generated islands with real Japanese ones, which are the owner's north star for the land; `foundations.md` section 11 has the target figures. Judge a change to the land against them.
- `docs/adr/`: why the engine is shaped as it is. Read before changing level of detail or lighting.
- `reference/README.md`: index of the earlier projects' idea documents, kept verbatim.

The sibling folders `../odincraft-v1` to `v4` and `../zigcraft*` hold the earlier attempts. Read their code only to learn what a feature did for the player. Design every implementation fresh.

## Working on the code

- Keep it small. The whole game is about 6,950 lines of Odin; prefer changing a rule to adding a system.
- What the world is lives in four files: `rock.odin` draws the rock under the island from the story its kind names and the seed, `land.odin` forms the island in that rock, `rivers.odin` traces and cuts the rivers, `gen.odin` answers the engine's questions from all three. `events.odin` holds what happened to the land and what that left: the features, a list on the region (`region.features`), each with a kind, place, extent, size, level, age in years, the feature that caused it and what was measured when it was made. `water.odin` then moves the water near the player.
- What kind of island it is lives in one record, `region` in `land.odin`: size, origin, outline, uplift pattern, rock story and history as a list of phases, drawn from the seed within the ranges of a row in `KINDS` (ridge, inlets, cone, crater, domes, worn, raised). A phase can open vents that build rock over it (a cone of runny lava and ash, or a dome of stiff lava, by the row's `form`) and whose tops can fall into closed craters; the volcanic kinds start from bare sea floor and are histories of such events, and their rock's beds lie over what the vents built. Change a kind by editing its row, never by adding a constant. `--seed=N` picks the world and the seed picks the kind, each row in proportion to its `weight`, so the inhospitable cone and crater are each drawn about one time in fourteen; `--kind=<name>` shows any kind on any seed. Seed 7, the default, draws the raised island. `--map` with `--overlay=<field>` paints one field.
- Rows are written in named fields, `FIELDS` in `fields.odin`: each has a name, a unit, what it comes from and how to read it at a point, and `--overlay=` paints any of them. A row's host is a list of windows, each a field with a range and an edge, which multiply; a row holds only names and literals, and a condition of a new shape becomes a new field. What covers the ground is the ordered rows `SURFACES` in `gen.odin`, the first whose host holds winning. Where soil holds, the plant community decides the rest: seven rows in `COMMUNITIES` (`gen.odin`), two of them the pioneers of ground a slide laid bare, compete for each 16 m cell in a weighted race (`race`, `fields.odin`) run once after forming, and each brings a tree density (the share of places in the canopy a tree takes), shares of the species in `SPECIES` (bark, leaf, crown form, size) and a ground cover. How old each stand of trees is lives in the field `stand-age` (`land.age`, drawn by `stand_draw` in `gen.odin` from the row `STANDS`): stands round middles about 140 m apart, each aged by the years since a typhoon threw its canopy, drawn more often where `exposure` is high, or by the slide's age on its ground. `trees_in` thins candidates on a 3 m grid to the stand's spacing (2.5 m new to 6.5 m old): the most vigorous within reach is a canopy tree, others stand between or under it, and gaps (`gap_at`) hold fallen trunks, standing dead ones and young growth. Only canopy trees and those between them are drawn beyond the block volume. Each tree is grown in blocks by one procedure, `grow` (`gen.odin`), from its species' row in `SPECIES`: stems (one, or a clump), a fork into limbs or a leader the limbs leave in tiers (the pine), a lobe of leaves at each limb's end, a lean toward the open (`open` in `Tree`, from stronger neighbours, gaps, the slope and the wind) and a cut on the windward side. Tune a species by its row, never by a branch in `grow`. The species' envelope (`envelope`) is the contract between near and far: `grow` keeps every leaf inside it, and the crowns drawn from 270 m to 1.1 km are that envelope round the same middle (`crown_at`), so a change to a species' shape goes through its envelope and is looked at across that hand-over. Leaves stay 3 m over the ground beneath them and a bush is a dome a walker can see over; nothing but stems stands lower. `TREE_REACH` (6) bounds a crown's radius plus its lean, and is the margin every caller searches for trees. What lies under the cover is the ordered rows `SOILS`, down to the depth in the `soil-depth` field (`land_local`, `land.odin`). The overlays `plants`, `soil`, `soil-depth`, `woods` and `stand-age` paint them, and start-up prints the share of the land each community, soil and age of stand takes. `build/odincraft --why=x,z` (in the world) forms the island and prints what the ground is there and why, the nearest miss, the plant community with its runner-up in the race and what held that back, the trees, the stand's age and what set it, the nearest tree and its place in the stand, and the soil, then exits.
- What stands on the ground between the trees is the ground plants (`plants.odin`): fifteen rows in `GROUND_PLANTS`, each with what it grows on, the communities it grows in, the share of columns it takes, the size of its clumps, the water it can stand in and a host on the fields (among them `canopy`, the sky the stand's crowns hide, and `over-water`, height over a lake or slow stream); the first row whose draw comes up at a column wins, decided in `gen_columns` at the nearest level (`Columns.plant`). A plant is two pictures crossed in an X on its column, drawn only from the nearest ring: the block mesher (`mesh_volume`) emits it where the ground block is still the one it grows on and air is over it, into a second buffer on the chunk (`Chunk.plants`), drawn after the terrain by `vs_plant`/`fs_plant` with alpha to coverage, lit as the ground is, casting no shadow and stopping nothing. They thin out from 70 to 190 m (`PLANT_NEAR`, `PLANT_FAR` in `shaders.metal`, and `PLANT_FAR` again in `render`), and water that rises over one past its row's `wet` takes it out when that chunk's water surface is rebuilt (`plants_drowned`). `--why=` names the plant at a point. The reeds of the default island are on the slide lake's shores near 150, -760; a gap full of bracken in the old stand is at 68, -1696.
- One event happens so far, a landslide (`SLIDE` and `slide_make` in `events.odin`), after the history and then worn for its age with the water behind it kept as a lake. It falls where its host holds best, a row of windows on the fields `at-limit`, `cap`, `valley` and `cold`; its size, how far its debris runs and its age are drawn from the seed. It leaves a scar and hummocky debris (the field `slid`, metres laid or, under 0, taken), the lake its debris dams and the ponds among its hummocks, each a feature; plants read its age through the field `disturbed`. The start-up line beginning "a slide" says where it is, how large and old, and what it dammed, or that the island has no host; `--overlay=slid` paints it, the plain map tints its debris ochre and its scar pale, and `--why=` on it names the feature. A lake or pond is water at one level over its 16 m cells and the cells beside them (`lake_near`); beyond them the ground stands as a bank, and far off a quad at the level over the same cells joins the rivers' strip. Near the player a lake starts full and is not topped up, so a breach drains it; the stream running in holds its level. On the default island the slide is 460 years old and its lake, 7.2 ha, lies near 150, -760; for a young slide with bare scar, raw debris and drowned trunks see `--kind=ridge --seed=2` near -1,660, 130.
- Roofed places are features too (`HOLLOWS` and `hollows_make` in `events.odin`): sea notches, rock shelters and sea caves, each placed where its row's host ranks best and kept as an outline, the floor, roof and ground top of every metre in its square (`hollows`). A new kind of volume feature is a row there and a shape in `hollow_carve`. `gen_columns` reads the outline into `Columns.hollow`, `gen_volume` empties each column from floor to roof, and `--why=` names the place. A face under a roof is lit by the share of sky its air sees (`sky_seen`, `mesh.odin`), kept in bits 4 to 7 of the quad's `info` as 15 less the light; at far levels the same bits darken a mouth's band on the risers (`mesh_columns`), and `fs_terrain` reads them in one line. Where the sea's level is air under rock, water stands on the floor beneath (`column_floor`) and is drawn dark. Start-up prints how many there are and where; the cone on seed 3 has all three (a notch at -880, 1145, a sea cave at -1625, -104 whose mouth faces west), and the default island a shelter under grit at 1703, 1080 whose mouth faces south. `--pos` places the feet, 1.6 m under the eye: in a notch put them under the water.
- What lies at the water's edge is `waters_edge` in `gen.odin`, which `ground` calls near the shore, from two fields on the 16 m grid made in `land_local`: `surf`, the swell of the nearest shore within 48 m of it on land or at sea, and the sand the rivers bring (`land.supply`, 320 m along the shore from each mouth), which less four times the surf is `sand-kept`. Where sand is kept it lies as a beach, a flat berm wider the more is kept and a face of 1 in 16; elsewhere the waves throw the island's own broken rock into a steeper, higher shingle bank; neither stands on ground steeper than about 1.5 or where surf passes 0.25. Where surf passes 0.1, the platform the forming left 2 m under the sea is raised to the sea's level, with gullies and pools along the strike of the beds, and boulders lie in heaps along the foot of what stands behind it. The depth laid is the field `beach`, and the surface rows `sand`, `boulders`, `shingle`, `washed rock` (bare to 1 m plus 8 m per unit of surf: the field `washed`) and `sea floor` read it. Sand is black on basalt and scoria (`SAND_OF`), beach and shore soil alike. Sea stacks are the row `.Stack` in `HOLLOWS` (`events.odin`), ranked as the notches are, each a list of `pillars` (the stack and up to three skerries) that `stack_at` raises in `ground` at every cell size, each bed standing out by its strength; `--why=` names a stack, and the map paints the shore in its materials and rings stacks yellow. On the default island sand lies near -1,780, 960 and the raised platform near 1,320, -1,568; the cone on seed 3 has a stack at -1,383, 646 (see it with `--kind=cone --seed=3 --fly --above=-1282,1.7,720 --look=300,5`), the ridge on seed 3 a pocket beach crossed by a river at -1,900, -280, and the crater on seed 3 black sand near -1,265, -60.
- Animals leave trails (`trails.odin`), laid once after the plants. The animal is a row, `DEER`, whose host is the island: sika deer live only where the sea has risen 100 m or more since it stood lowest (the field `risen`), so of today's kinds only inlets has them; every other island, the default among them, prints "no large animal" and has none (`docs/interesting-world/notes/real-animals.md`). `pace` is the one cost of crossing ground (the field `crossing`, seconds a metre: Tobler's hiking function on the grade, a wall past 45°, the plants by the table `GOING`, faster where a trail is worn), and `trace` the one route tracer over the 16 m cells, adding the time to wade a stream (cheap only on a river's lip). A bed in each 500 m square goes to water, to open ground and to one neighbouring bed; each route and each stream crossing is a feature (`.Trail`, `.Ford`), and the network is smoothed into segments filed in buckets like the rivers', found by `trail_near`. In blocks a trail is the surface row `trail` (bare earth, the field `trail`, out to 2 m cells, so about 512 m) and no tree or bush stands within 2.5 m of it (one line in `trees_in`); the map draws trails red and fords white, and `--why=` names the trail and the ford. To see them, use `--kind=inlets`: on seed 3 a ford where seven routes meet lies at -2,407, -184.
- The sky is drawn by the renderer and never baked into the terrain (`render.odin`, `shaders.metal`). `sun_dir` gives the sun for an hour of solar time and a day of the year at `region.latitude` (30° N), and the `sunlight` field reads the same procedure. `--day=N` sets the day (0 is 1 January; default: the first fair day from 80, the March equinox, on, which is day 95 on the default island; pass `--weather=0` for a picture that must have a clear sky); `--hour=` and `[` `]` turn the clock. The weather is the row `SKY` in `render.odin`: each month's mean cloud cover from the sunshine at Tanegashima (30.7° N), and spells of two days, each drawing a cover around that mean and a cloud base; `weather_at` gives both for a day and hour, the same for the same seed. The clouds are one layer 300 m deep from the cloud base, a field of noise drifting downwind from the swell's bearing at 8 m/s and covering the share of the sky the cover says, plus a cap where the field `lifted` (how high the sea wind has been raised by the ground it crossed) passes the cloud base. The same field shades the land and water under it, and the sea reflects it; a camera above the base sees the tops. `--weather=c[,b]` forces the cover (0 clear to 1 overcast) and the base (default 1,000 m, too high for a cap): judge the land with `--weather=0`, and see a cap with a low base, for example `--kind=cone --weather=0,450 --pos=-2300,25,-3800 --look=149,6`. Start-up prints the day's noon sun, its daylight and the drawn weather. No snow falls here: see `foundations.md`, step 10.
- Rivers are sized by discharge, `discharge(area)` in `rivers.odin` (`RUNOFF` of the rain runs off; the field `discharge` paints it): a brook a block wide where a stream starts, 3.5 m and knee deep at 1 km², 9 m at 7 km². Falls are made in `falls_make`: where a stream is steeper than `FALL_STEEP` over a strong bed, the bed holds a lip and the stream drops in one fall to the next strong bed or the foot of the steep ground (at a sea cliff, to the sea), and runs below it in a gorge whose walls rise from the water (`Segment.gorge`); every drop over `FALL_MIN` (2.5 m) is a feature `.Fall` with its height, the land drained and the depth of the pool it scoured (`plunge`), deepened into the bed. The falling water is one set of quads, `rivers.falls`, built once and drawn near and far by the simulated water's pipeline; the near simulation draws no sheet into a fall's pool and holds every river column at its level both ways, so what crosses a lip leaves through the fall. A spring (`.Spring`) is where a bed in `PASSES` lies on one that holds water, in a hollow on a valley side with `SPRING_AREA` above it; a brook starts there. Start-up prints the falls by height and the tallest, and the springs; the plain map marks falls white and springs cyan, and `--why=` near either names it. The default island's tallest is 97 m near -1,212, 442 and the one in the sheet is 86 m at -252, -327; the cone on seed 3 has 124 m to the sea at -788, -1,125.
- Named places live in `places.odin`, run last at start-up (`places_make`). A finder gathers candidates nature made (summits and the passes between them from one union-find sweep of the land's cells, faces, headlands, the crater, the oldest wood, and the features: falls, lakes, the slide, caves, notches, shelters, springs), each with a measure against its row's worth in `PLACE_ROWS`; then lines of sight from 120 viewpoints 1 to 2 km round say how much of the ground sees each. A candidate's score is how far past its worth (log scale), shared among the others of its kind nearly as extreme (not for summits), times how well seen and how large; at 1 or more, best first, 900 m apart and at most three of a kind, it becomes a place (a `.Place` feature) taking in what lies within 400 m, named by `place_name` in plain English from its cause ("Tabu Tarn", "the Black Bowl"). Islands have 0 to 5; the default island has one, Tabu Tarn. Start-up lists them; `--map` numbers them; `--why=` names the place a point is at or the nearest; the window's title shows it; `--place=N` puts the camera at its viewpoint (with `--shot=` and `--weather=0` for a picture). `--survey` prints what the island holds (each community, rock and soil, absent, everywhere or in how many 1 km squares; features; extremes) and every candidate ranked with its score, then exits. Change what earns a name by a row's `worth`, not by a branch.
- What a block looks like is a tile in `assets/blocks.png`: a row for each material in the order of `Mat`, its top then its side, 16 pixels square, compiled into the game. The tiles are an artist's, from the Pixel Perfection pack in `assets/pixel-perfection/` (CC BY-SA 4.0; `assets/LICENSE.md` has the attribution, which must stay). `tools/blocks.py` builds the atlas: its `TILES` table names the tile each material takes, and each is recoloured to the material's colour in `MATERIALS` (run it from the repository root with a Python that has Pillow; with a file name it also writes an enlarged sheet to look at). After adding a material, name its tile there and run it, or the game stops at start-up. Do not generate tiles in code: the owner rejected that twice (`docs/interesting-world/notes/block-textures.md`). The plants are those of the warm islands of southern Japan (`docs/interesting-world/notes/real-vegetation.md`): judge a change to them against that note.
- Everything in the region is stored relative to its middle, and `--origin=x,z` places that middle in the world (default 0,0); the generator's entry points (`gen_columns`, `gen_volume`) and the start convert. Positions given to `--above`, `--pos` and the bench's printed end point are in the world.
- Judge a change to the land from the map first: `build/odincraft --map=out.png` forms the island, writes a 1024-pixel relief map with rivers and exits, in about a second.
- `./build.sh` builds with unused-code checks on. `./build.sh run` plays.
- A run with `--shot` or `--bench` opens no window and takes no focus: it draws unseen, at full size, and a bench paces its frames as a 120 Hz display would, so its "frame interval" line can be trusted whatever else is on screen. Never add `--show` (which opens the window) unless the owner asks to watch: the owner works on this machine, and several agents may be testing at once. Benches run at the same time slow each other, so repeat one that fails before believing it.
- Look at every visual change yourself before showing it: `build/odincraft --shot=out.png` waits for the world to load, writes one 2560 × 1440 frame and exits. Add `--fly`, and place the camera with `--above=x,height,z` (height over the ground) or `--pos=x,y,z`, `--look=yaw,pitch` in degrees and `--hour=`. The region is 8,192 m square around its origin, and the default raised island spans about -2,600 to 2,950 in x and -2,300 to 2,700 in z of it; the map shows the whole region at 8 m a pixel with -z at the top, and yaw 0 looks toward -z. Other kinds and seeds put the land elsewhere: read the map first. The PNG is uncompressed; shrink it with `sips -Z 1600 -s format jpeg` before reading it.
- A fault that shows only while moving needs a frame from mid-flight: `--bench=30 --fly --speed=200 --pos=... --shot=out.png --after=3.3` saves the frame at that second without waiting for the world to load.
- Check frame pacing after any change to drawing or streaming: `build/odincraft --bench=15 --fly --speed=120 --above=-900,60,1700 --look=90,0`, which crosses the default island's south flank over forest. The target is no frame over 12.5 ms. A bench also prints the faces and ground plants drawn a frame. Without `--fly` the same option runs along the ground, which exercises collision, and prints how far it got.
- After any change to the land, the rock or trees, fly fast across the steepest ground of each kind, since steep land finds bugs gentle land hides: `build/odincraft --bench=20 --fly --speed=200 --above=0,80,-2000 --look=180,0` crosses the default island from north to south over its old sea cliff and upland, and `--kind=` with a path read off the map, or through the tallest face the start-up lines name, covers the others: for example `--kind=crater --seed=3 --bench=20 --fly --speed=200 --pos=-1400,330,30 --look=90,0` crosses a crater rim and floor, and `--bench=10 --fly --speed=100 --above=-300,40,-700 --look=95,0` crosses the default island's slide and its lakes. Over the sea `--above` measures from a sea floor 200 m down; start a flight or a view from the sea with `--pos`.
- After any change to the player, trees or the start, run `build/odincraft --bench=8` with no position: that is how the owner starts, on a river bank that no lake has drowned, with no canopy tree within 6 m (3 m where every bank is wooded closer) and no small one within 2 m (near 527, -1,061 on the default island, in valley forest south of the slide's lake, since narrower rivers brought the old bank in grass within 6 m of a tree; a bank by a fall, a cascade or in a gorge is never chosen), and about 31 m is the expected distance.
- The start-up lines print the island's kind, the bearing its swell comes from and its history, its rock, how long it took to form (keep it near one second) and how long a step cut at the coast takes to climb to the farthest stream head; the years the land has had on its finest cells against that says how finished it is. The last three lines are the figures the real islands were measured by, each with the real islands' range beside it, ending with livable ground (the `livable` overlay paints it), sheltered landings and the deepest closed hollow: judge a change to the land by them.
- A dark or odd patch in a screenshot is usually real geometry lit truthfully. Move the camera to it before changing the mesher.
- Place cameras with `--above`, not `--pos`, near the ground. A camera inside the ground sees sky through the terrain and looks like missing water or missing land.
- Water takes time to move, and its animation drifts over time. Test it late and with numbers: `--after=45` delays a screenshot, `--dig=x0,y0,z0,x1,y1,z1` clears a box of blocks once the world has loaded (repeatable; `--after` then counts from the dig), and `--probe=x0,z0,x1,z1` prints ground, water depth and whether each column is supplied when the game exits. Probe across a river first to find its banks.
