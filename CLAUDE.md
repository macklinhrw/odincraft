# OdinCraft v5

A small first-person block game used to test directions for world generation, look and gameplay. The owner decides direction and judges everything visual by looking; record each decision in `docs/open-decisions.md` the moment it is made.

- `docs/ideas.md`: read before proposing a feature.
- `docs/lessons.md`: read before proposing architecture, a plan or a way of working.
- `docs/foundations.md`: the plan for the wider world and the order of work on the island; one design per system in `docs/foundations/`. Read its sections 3 and 8 before adding to the land, rock, water, plants or living things. A plan, not decisions.
- `docs/interesting-world.md`: the research under that plan. Its note `interesting-world/notes/real-islands.md` compares the generated islands with real Japanese ones, which are the owner's north star for the land; `foundations.md` section 11 has the target figures. Judge a change to the land against them.
- `docs/adr/`: why the engine is shaped as it is. Read before changing level of detail or lighting.
- `reference/README.md`: index of the earlier projects' idea documents, kept verbatim.

The sibling folders `../odincraft-v1` to `v4` and `../zigcraft*` hold the earlier attempts. Read their code only to learn what a feature did for the player. Design every implementation fresh.

## Working on the code

- Keep it small. The whole game is about 8,750 lines of Odin; prefer changing a rule to adding a system.
- What the world is lives in four files: `rock.odin` draws the rock under the island from the story its kind names and the seed, `land.odin` forms the island in that rock, `rivers.odin` traces and cuts the rivers, `gen.odin` answers the engine's questions from all three. `events.odin` holds what happened to the land and what that left: the features, a list on the region (`region.features`), each with a kind, place, extent, size, level, age in years, the feature that caused it and what was measured when it was made. `water.odin` then moves the water near the player.
- The world holds every island of the archipelago at once. The islands are records (`Island` in `archipelago.odin`: middle, region size, kind and seed), made from the world's seed by `archipelago_make`; until step 17 lands it is a stand-in of three, island 0 the default island at the origin, island 1 a cone 14 km east (14,000, -3,000) and island 2 the inlets kind 16 km south (-6,000, 15,000). Everything formed for an island is one record, `Formed` (`regions.odin`), and each system still reads its part under its own name (`region`, `land`, `rivers`, `rocks`, `trails`, `fauna`, `hollows`, `places` and the rest, and `seed`): each is a thread-local pointer that `bind` points at one island's record, so "the region" in these notes means the region of the island in hand. A thread binds before it reads: forming an island (`gen_init`), building a chunk (`gen_columns` binds per column, `gen_volume` the island under the chunk's middle), posing creatures (each island within 3 km), `--why`, the map and the start (the start island, which the frame thread keeps bound; `bound` binds until the end of a scope). A new system keeps its state in `Formed` and adds its pointer to `bind`; a package-level variable that forming writes is a race. Between the regions lies the open sea, its floor at `SEA_BED` as every region's rim is (`island_at`, `height_in_world`). The start island (`--island=N`, default 0) says all its start-up lines; every island within 64 km of it (`--near=km`) is formed beside it, side by side on all cores but one, before entering, each saying one line once all are done; the rest on three low-priority threads in the background, nearest first, each saying one line when done. Entering takes about 1.4 s with 64 km (0.7 s with 24, 0.6 s with the start island alone), held by the largest island near the start. Work inside one island is shared among threads wherever each cell's result stands alone (`shared`, `regions.odin`): the thread forming the island and every idle helper take parts of the loop, and a part comes out the same on any thread, so an island is the same bit for bit alone, beside others and on any number of threads. A loop whose passes depend on their order stays on the forming thread: the flood in `route`, which is half of forming, `erode` and `coast`. An island formed beside play works alone. A chunk over an island not yet formed is not built until it is (`waits`, `world.odin`). Each island keeps about 40 MB once formed. `--map`, `--why`, `--survey`, `--place=N`, `--kind=` and the start act on the island `--island=` names, and form only it when they exit at once; the weather reads the world's seed (`world_seed`), the clouds drift from the start island's swell, and the cloud cap is the nearest island's `lifted`.
- What kind of island it is lives in one record, `region` in `land.odin`: size, origin, outline, uplift pattern, rock story and history as a list of phases, drawn from the seed within the ranges of a row in `KINDS` (ridge, inlets, cone, crater, domes, worn, raised). A phase can open vents that build rock over it (a cone of runny lava and ash, or a dome of stiff lava, by the row's `form`) and whose tops can fall into closed craters; the volcanic kinds start from bare sea floor and are histories of such events, and their rock's beds lie over what the vents built. A volcano's rock story has a `mantle` (`STORIES`, `rock.odin`): ash creeps over its lava wherever it can lie, which is not on a face, so its flanks are smooth between a few deep ravines. Change a kind by editing its row, never by adding a constant. `--seed=N` picks the world and the seed picks the kind, each row in proportion to its `weight`, so the inhospitable cone and crater are each drawn about one time in fourteen; `--kind=<name>` shows any kind on any seed. Seed 7, the default, draws the raised island. `--map` with `--overlay=<field>` paints one field.
- Today's island is one of the islands near the start, in a world with continents, and those islands and the continent they lie off are records (`archipelago.odin`), made by `archipelago_make` (about 1 ms) from the start island's record, drawn first at start-up, and changing nothing on today's island; `world_at` reads the world's seed (`world_seed`), never an island's. Where they stand comes from a fixed stand-in for the world's plates near the start, the row `PLATES`: a trench curved as an arc of a circle, the arc's volcanic front set back from it, between them the forearc rising fastest along its outer ridge, behind the front a trough, then the shelf, the drowned margin of a continent, and its coast; on the plate going down, a hotspot whose islands age along the plate's motion toward the trench and sink as they cool. It makes the world's fields `setting`, `front`, `trench`, `track`, `age`, `rising` and `depth` (`world_at`; `--overlay=` paints them over today's island). The stand-in is a placeholder for step 22 of `docs/foundations.md`, the world's plate story, which replaces it: a plate history must give the same fields in the same units (the kind rows' hosts read `front`, `rising`, `age` and `depth`), each island's place, size and age, the continent's coast, and the wind. The start island keeps the kind the seed draws, and the plates are put so that kind's host holds where it stands. 10 to 30 other islands are placed along the front, across the forearc, along the hotspot's track and on the shelf, their regions 8 to 16 km across by Korčák's law (smaller regions form islands that do not read as their kind; the place finder's survey still assumes 8 km, so on a 16 km region it counts only the north-west quarter's 1 km squares); each is an `Island` in `archipelago` (its region's middle, size, kind, seed, setting and age), whose kind is the row in `KINDS` whose `hosts` (windows on those fields) it holds best, ties drawn by name, and never the kind of its nearest neighbour. Change where a kind of island stands by its row's `hosts`, never by a branch. A site no kind holds is left out and printed, as over the hotspot itself, where a young shield would stand and no row makes one. Every island's swell and wind come from one bearing, the start island's own. The continent is a record too (`continent`): its coast for 300 km round the start, closed far inland, and the crest of a range behind it whose height is a guess; none of it is formed until step 21 finds how. `build/odincraft --archipelago=out.png` (with `--seed=` and `--kind=` as usual) forms every island side by side on the game's threads (`islands_form`, `regions.odin`), about 3 s for seed 7's 17, writes a grey map 200 km across with the trench, the front, the shelf's edge, the hotspot's track ticked each million years, the continent's coast and range, each island named by number, kind and setting, and prints a line for each island, what was left out, and whether each island sees its nearest neighbour and the continent's range over the curve.
- Rows are written in named fields, `FIELDS` in `fields.odin`: each has a name, a unit, what it comes from and how to read it at a point, and `--overlay=` paints any of them. A row's host is a list of windows, each a field with a range and an edge, which multiply; a row holds only names and literals, and a condition of a new shape becomes a new field. What covers the ground is the ordered rows `SURFACES` in `gen.odin`, the first whose host holds winning. Where soil holds, the plant community decides the rest: seven rows in `COMMUNITIES` (`gen.odin`), two of them the pioneers of ground a slide laid bare, compete for each 16 m cell in a weighted race (`race`, `fields.odin`) run once after forming, and each brings a tree density (the share of places in the canopy a tree takes), shares of the species in `SPECIES` (bark, leaf, crown form, size) and a ground cover. How old each stand of trees is lives in the field `stand-age` (`land.age`, drawn by `stand_draw` in `gen.odin` from the row `STANDS`): stands round middles about 140 m apart, each aged by the years since a typhoon threw its canopy, drawn more often where `exposure` is high, or by the slide's age on its ground. `trees_in` thins candidates on a 3 m grid to the stand's spacing (3.5 m new to 7.5 m old): the most vigorous within reach is a canopy tree, others stand between or under it, and gaps (`gap_at`) hold young growth and, rarely, a fallen trunk (a stump and a log lying straight along the grid, 3 to 7 m) or a broken dead stub: one thrown tree in ten of each (`STANDS.fallen`), which is one or two logs to a hectare of old wood. The owner twice found fallen trees too common (2026-11-12). The owner found the woods too thick to walk and the fallen trees unreadable (2026-11-12): keep a walker's way between trunks, and thin by a row's `density` and `bushes` or by `STANDS`. Only canopy trees and those between them are drawn beyond the block volume. Each tree is grown in blocks by one procedure, `grow` (`gen.odin`), from its species' row in `SPECIES`: one stem (clumps of two or three a block apart read as a wall), a fork into limbs or a leader the limbs leave in tiers (the pine), a lobe of leaves at each limb's end, a lean toward the open (`open` in `Tree`, from stronger neighbours, gaps, the slope and the wind) and a cut on the windward side. Leaves close over the end of every stem and limb, so no trunk stands bare-topped. Near by (72 m, `LEAF_FAR` in `render.odin` and `shaders.metal`) leaves are seen through: the gaps in their artist's tiles are clear in the atlas, each block of leaves shows behind the one in front, with the limbs among them (`LEAVES`, `mesh_volume`). A chunk's buffer holds its solid faces, then its crowns' outer faces, then one dim face for each pair of leaf blocks that meet; the last two are drawn from both sides with alpha to coverage by the pipeline `g.leaf`, nearest chunk first, the inside of a crown in its own shade. Farther off only the outer faces are drawn, whole, by the terrain's pipeline, the gaps in the dark shade the atlas keeps under them. The owner asked for both: leaves seen through, and layered on each other, not joined into one block (2026-11-12). Every layer of see-through leaves can be shaded in full, so do not widen `LEAF_FAR` without the canopy bench (`--bench=12 --fly --speed=30 --above=-600,12,900 --look=180,0`). Tune a species by its row, never by a branch in `grow`. Bark has a grain (`grain`, `mesh.odin`): the mesher reads it from the blocks round a face, so a stump or a cut trunk shows rings on top, and a log on the ground shows rings at its ends, bark turned along it and moss on top, whether the world or the player laid it; the ring and turned-bark tiles are materials after the barks in `Mat` that no block is ever made of. The species' envelope (`envelope`) is the contract between near and far: `grow` keeps every leaf inside it, and the crowns drawn from 270 m to 1.1 km are that envelope round the same middle (`crown_at`), so a change to a species' shape goes through its envelope and is looked at across that hand-over. Leaves stay 3 m over the ground beneath them and a bush is a dome a walker can see over; nothing but stems stands lower. `TREE_REACH` (6) bounds a crown's radius plus its lean, and is the margin every caller searches for trees. What lies under the cover is the ordered rows `SOILS`, down to the depth in the `soil-depth` field (`land_local`, `land.odin`). The overlays `plants`, `soil`, `soil-depth`, `woods` and `stand-age` paint them, and start-up prints the share of the land each community, soil and age of stand takes. `build/odincraft --why=x,z` (in the world) forms the island and prints what the ground is there and why, the nearest miss, the plant community with its runner-up in the race and what held that back, the trees, the stand's age and what set it, the nearest tree and its place in the stand, and the soil, then exits.
- What stands on the ground between the trees is the ground plants (`plants.odin`): fifteen rows in `GROUND_PLANTS`, each with what it grows on, the communities it grows in, the share of columns it takes, the size of its clumps, the water it can stand in and a host on the fields (among them `canopy`, the sky the stand's crowns hide, and `over-water`, height over a lake or slow stream); the first row whose draw comes up at a column wins, decided in `gen_columns` at the nearest level (`Columns.plant`). A plant is two pictures crossed in an X on its column, drawn only from the nearest ring: the block mesher (`mesh_volume`) emits it where the ground block is still the one it grows on and air is over it, into a second buffer on the chunk (`Chunk.plants`), drawn after the terrain by `vs_plant`/`fs_plant` with alpha to coverage, lit as the ground is, casting no shadow and stopping nothing. They thin out from 70 to 190 m (`PLANT_NEAR`, `PLANT_FAR` in `shaders.metal`, and `PLANT_FAR` again in `render`), and water that rises over one past its row's `wet` takes it out when that chunk's water surface is rebuilt (`plants_drowned`). `--why=` names the plant at a point. The reeds of the default island are on the slide lake's shores near 150, -760; a gap full of bracken in the old stand is at 68, -1696.
- Rock shows in two ways (`gen.odin`). Where a strong bed crosses steep ground it stands as a face with a tread on its top (`ledges`), but only in stretches a few hundred metres long (`stands`): most of the way along grit, about a third of the way along sandstone, and between them the hillside runs smooth through the bed under scrub, so a hill wears crags and bluffs and not a wall round it at every strong bed. The owner found the walls read as a mesa (2026-11-12). And ground is bare wherever it is steeper than soil holds (`SOIL_HOLDS`, a rise over run of 1.3, about 52°).
- One event happens so far, a landslide (`SLIDE` and `slide_make` in `events.odin`), after the history and then worn for its age with the water behind it kept as a lake. It falls where its host holds best, a row of windows on the fields `at-limit`, `cap`, `valley` and `cold`; its size, how far its debris runs and its age are drawn from the seed. It leaves a scar and hummocky debris (the field `slid`, metres laid or, under 0, taken), the lake its debris dams and the ponds among its hummocks, each a feature; plants read its age through the field `disturbed`. The start-up line beginning "a slide" says where it is, how large and old, and what it dammed, or that the island has no host; `--overlay=slid` paints it, the plain map tints its debris ochre and its scar pale, and `--why=` on it names the feature. A lake or pond is water at one level over its 16 m cells and the cells beside them (`lake_near`); beyond them the ground stands as a bank, and far off a quad at the level over the same cells joins the rivers' strip. Near the player a lake starts full and is not topped up, so a breach drains it; the stream running in holds its level. On the default island the slide is 460 years old and its lake, 7.2 ha, lies near 150, -760; for a young slide with bare scar, raw debris and drowned trunks see `--kind=ridge --seed=2` near -1,660, 130.
- Roofed places are features too (`HOLLOWS` and `hollows_make` in `events.odin`): sea notches, rock shelters and sea caves, each placed where its row's host ranks best and kept as an outline, the floor, roof and ground top of every metre in its square (`hollows`). A new kind of volume feature is a row there and a shape in `hollow_carve`. `gen_columns` reads the outline into `Columns.hollow`, `gen_volume` empties each column from floor to roof, and `--why=` names the place. A face under a roof is lit by the share of sky its air sees (`sky_seen`, `mesh.odin`), kept in bits 4 to 7 of the quad's `info` as 15 less the light; at far levels the same bits darken a mouth's band on the risers (`mesh_columns`), and `fs_terrain` reads them in one line. Where the sea's level is air under rock, water stands on the floor beneath (`column_floor`) and is drawn dark. Start-up prints how many there are and where; the cone on seed 3 has all three (a notch at -1112, 952, a sea cave at -1592, -440), and the default island a shelter under grit at 1703, 1080 whose mouth faces south. `--pos` places the feet, 1.6 m under the eye: in a notch put them under the water.
- What lies at the water's edge is `waters_edge` in `gen.odin`, which `ground` calls near the shore, from two fields on the 16 m grid made in `land_local`: `surf`, the swell of the nearest shore within 48 m of it on land or at sea, and the sand the rivers bring (`land.supply`, 320 m along the shore from each mouth), which less two and a half times the surf (`CARRY`) is `sand-kept`. Where sand is kept it lies as a beach, a flat berm wider the more is kept and a face of 1 in 16; elsewhere the waves throw the island's own broken rock into a steeper, higher shingle bank; neither stands on ground steeper than about 1.5 or where surf passes 0.25. Shingle is its own material of sea-worn pebbles (dark on basalt, `shore_of`), not the cliff's scree. Only under steep ground (a slope over about 0.45 where surf passes 0.1) do the waves leave a bench at the sea's level, a cell or so wide, with gullies and pools along the strike of the beds; a gentle shore has none, and the rock the forming cut 2 m under the sea stays under it, covered by sand below 1.5 m. The owner found the wide shelf of rock that once fronted every open shore made no sense (2026-11-12). Boulders lie in heaps along the foot of what stands over an open shore. A column the land says is under the sea is drawn under it (`gen_columns`), so a shoal is a shallow and not a raft of sand. The depth laid is the field `beach`, and the surface rows `sand`, `boulders`, `shingle`, `washed rock` (bare to 1 m plus 8 m per unit of surf: the field `washed`) and `sea floor` read it. Sand is black on basalt and scoria (`SAND_OF`), beach and shore soil alike. Sea stacks are the row `.Stack` in `HOLLOWS` (`events.odin`), ranked as the notches are, each a list of `pillars` (the stack and up to three skerries) that `stack_at` raises in `ground` at every cell size, each bed standing out by its strength; `--why=` names a stack, and the map paints the shore in its materials and rings stacks yellow. On the default island sand lies near -1,780, 960 and a rocky open shore near -930, -2,170; the cone on seed 3 has a stack at -926, 1,169 (see it with `--kind=cone --seed=3 --fly --pos=-1040,3,1330 --look=35,8`), the ridge on seed 3 a pocket beach crossed by a river at -1,900, -280, and the crater on seed 3 black sand near -1,265, -60.
- What lives on an island is never the owner's preference, or yours: an animal or plant is there because it could reach the island (the island's story), the island can keep it (its host on the fields) and, where arrival is a matter of chance, the seed drew it. Never ask the owner whether an island should have a thing; give the thing a row that decides, and print why not.
- Animals leave trails (`trails.odin`), laid once after the plants. The animal is a row, `DEER`: sika deer live only where the sea has risen 100 m or more since it stood lowest (the field `risen`: they walked there), and where the island can keep a herd (`herd`: six 500 m squares with sheltered wood to lie up in, with fresh water and open ground to feed on). Of today's kinds only inlets has them; every other island, the default among them, prints "no large animal" and why (`docs/interesting-world/notes/real-animals.md`). Arrival by swimming, which is chance and depends on how far the nearest larger land is, waits for the islands to have neighbours (Phase 2). `pace` is the one cost of crossing ground (the field `crossing`, seconds a metre: Tobler's hiking function on the grade, a wall past 45°, the plants by the table `GOING`, faster where a trail is worn), and `trace` the one route tracer over the 16 m cells, adding the time to wade a stream (cheap only on a river's lip). A bed in each 500 m square goes to water, to open ground and to one neighbouring bed; each route and each stream crossing is a feature (`.Trail`, `.Ford`), and the network is smoothed into segments filed in buckets like the rivers', found by `trail_near`. In blocks a trail is the surface row `trail` (bare earth, the field `trail`, out to 2 m cells, so about 512 m) and no tree or bush stands within 2.5 m of it (one line in `trees_in`); the map draws trails red and fords white, and `--why=` names the trail and the ford. To see them, use `--kind=inlets`: on seed 3 a ford where seven routes meet lies at -2,407, -184.
- Birds and fish (`creatures.odin`) are the only things drawn that move and are not blocks or water. Each kind is a row in `CREATURES` (black kite, osprey, ayu, yoshinobori, chosen in `docs/interesting-world/notes/real-creatures.md`): its home (a face the wind turns upward, the field `lift`; a sea stack; or streams and lakes), a host on the fields, numbers, season, its body as a few boxes (`Part`: middle, half size, a hinge, the back's colour and the flanks') and how it moves. Homes are chosen once after the places (`creatures_make`): a bird's is a feature (`.Haunt`) with a loop kept clear of the ground and trees; a fish lives in each segment of stream and each lake its host holds in, where the tallest fall between it and the sea (the field `barrier`) is within what it climbs. No animal is kept: each frame `creatures_pose` poses every one in reach from its kind, home, number and a clock of day, hour and seconds (in a `--shot`, the `--after` seconds, so the same seed and hour show the same birds, and another `--after` shows them moved on); only a startled fish remembers how far and which way it fled. The boxes are one instanced draw (`vs_box`/`fs_box`), lit as the terrain and casting no shadow; fish are drawn 2 to 2.5 times life size (`drawn`), since at life size they do not show. Change a kind by its row. Start-up prints what lives where, why not, and the best pool; `--why=` names the birds of a home and the fish of a stream or lake and why others are not there; the map rings bird homes pale blue and dots streams with fish green. A bench prints the boxes drawn. On the default island kites soar over the slide's scar (see one from below: `--fly --pos=-74,225,-689 --look=-45,34 --weather=0 --hour=10`), an osprey pair nests on the stack at 2,753, -395 (`--fly --pos=2749,15.5,-391 --look=48,-22`), and ayu (from day 90, full grown by about 180) and yoshinobori hold in the river south of the slide's lake (`--fly --pos=516.5,15,-1066.5 --look=95,-38 --day=200`); on `--kind=cone --seed=3` the ospreys' stack is 54 m high at -926, 1,169 under a kites' cliff (`--fly --pos=-1000,60,1500 --look=10,5`).
- The sky is drawn by the renderer and never baked into the terrain (`render.odin`, `shaders.metal`). `sun_dir` gives the sun for an hour of solar time and a day of the year at `region.latitude` (30° N), and the `sunlight` field reads the same procedure. `--day=N` sets the day (0 is 1 January; default: the first fair day from 80, the March equinox, on, which is day 95 on the default island; pass `--weather=0` for a picture that must have a clear sky); `--hour=` and `[` `]` turn the clock. The weather is the row `SKY` in `render.odin`: each month's mean cloud cover from the sunshine at Tanegashima (30.7° N), and spells of two days, each drawing a cover around that mean and a cloud base; `weather_at` gives both for a day and hour, the same for the same seed. The clouds are one layer 300 m deep from the cloud base, a field of noise drifting downwind from the swell's bearing at 8 m/s and covering the share of the sky the cover says, plus a cap where the field `lifted` (how high the sea wind has been raised by the ground it crossed) passes the cloud base. The same field shades the land and water under it, and the sea reflects it; a camera above the base sees the tops. `--weather=c[,b]` forces the cover (0 clear to 1 overcast) and the base (default 1,000 m, too high for a cap): judge the land with `--weather=0`, and see a cap with a low base, for example `--kind=cone --weather=0,450 --pos=-2300,25,-3800 --look=149,6`. Start-up prints the day's noon sun, its daylight and the drawn weather. No snow falls here: see `foundations.md`, step 10.
- The world is drawn round (`drawn` in `shaders.metal`, `adr/0006`): every vertex of the view is lowered by d²/2R as it is drawn, d its distance across the ground from the eye and R 7,400 km (`EARTH`), so 15 m at 15 km, 108 m at 40 km and 280 m at 64 km, and an eye 10 m over the sea has its horizon 12 km off. The physics, the generator, all shading and the shadow maps stay flat, so a new vertex shader for the view must call `drawn` and a shadow pass must not. The rings of chunks (`world.odin`) have cells of 1 to 256 m (`LEVELS`), 17 chunks a side, and reach from about 256 m (the block volume) to 65 km; a column wider than 64 m (`COARSE`, `gen.odin`) is the land's mean with no stream cut or stack in it, and under the sea it has no faces, since the sea is opaque past 16 km (`fs_water`), so a chunk of them wholly outside the region is not worked out at all (`open_sea`, `gen.odin`). A far chunk's flat run of tops in a row is one face. The sea beyond the simulated water is 64 × 64 quads round the eye, widening out past the horizon (`vs_water`), and far out it is not a mirror, so its horizon shows as a line. The haze thickens more slowly past a few kilometres than near by (`CLEAR`), so land 30 to 60 km off shows bluer and paler with its skyline; within 2 km it is as it was. See the island from the sea with `--pos` (the eye is 1.6 m over it, so `--pos=x,8.4,z` puts it 10 m over the water): `--fly --pos=-88,8.4,20392 --look=0,0 --weather=0` from 20 km south, and `--pos=-88,8.4,40392` from 40 km, where its lowest 52 m are under the horizon; from over the trees on its summit out to sea, `--fly --above=-88,30,392 --look=180,-2 --weather=0`, where the horizon is 75 km off. At 70° a 40 km island is 8 pixels high: crop the picture to judge it.
- Rivers are sized by discharge, `discharge(area)` in `rivers.odin` (`RUNOFF` of the rain runs off; the field `discharge` paints it): a brook a block wide where a stream starts, 3.5 m and knee deep at 1 km², 9 m at 7 km². Falls are made in `falls_make`: where a stream is steeper than `FALL_STEEP` over a strong bed, the bed holds a lip and the stream drops in one fall to the next strong bed or the foot of the steep ground (at a sea cliff, to the sea), and runs below it in a gorge whose walls rise from the water (`Segment.gorge`); every drop over `FALL_MIN` (2.5 m) is a feature `.Fall` with its height, the land drained and the depth of the pool it scoured (`plunge`), deepened into the bed. The falling water is one set of quads, `rivers.falls`, built once and drawn near and far by the simulated water's pipeline: a sheet as wide as its stream that begins on the stream a stride back from the lip, white strands with gaps where little water crosses (its quads say what they are with values below zero, which `vs_pool` reads; the simulated water's own are never negative). Each fall is also a `Drop` in `rivers.drops`, from which `spray` poses small white boxes each frame, thrown up where it lands and shed down its length, through the creatures' draw. A step under 2.5 m in a stream is the simulation's own sheet: the stream's water with thin white streaks, smooth above the lip and foaming below. the near simulation draws no sheet into a fall's pool and holds every river column at its level both ways, so what crosses a lip leaves through the fall. A spring (`.Spring`) is where a bed in `PASSES` lies on one that holds water, in a hollow on a valley side with `SPRING_AREA` above it; a brook starts there. Start-up prints the falls by height and the tallest, and the springs; the plain map marks falls white and springs cyan, and `--why=` near either names it. The default island's tallest is 97 m near -1,212, 442 and the one in the sheet is 86 m at -252, -327; the cone on seed 3 has 70 m into a gorge at 959, -1,102.
- Named places live in `places.odin`, run last at start-up (`places_make`). A finder gathers candidates nature made (summits and the passes between them from one union-find sweep of the land's cells, faces, headlands, the crater, the oldest wood, and the features: falls, lakes, the slide, caves, notches, shelters, springs), each with a measure against its row's worth in `PLACE_ROWS`; then lines of sight from 120 viewpoints 1 to 2 km round say how much of the ground sees each. A candidate's score is how far past its worth (log scale), shared among the others of its kind nearly as extreme (not for summits), times how well seen and how large; at 1 or more, best first, 900 m apart and at most three of a kind, it becomes a place (a `.Place` feature) taking in what lies within 400 m, named by `place_name` in plain English from its cause ("Tabu Tarn", "the Black Bowl"). Islands have 0 to 5; the default island has two, Tabu Tarn and Three Falls. A place no viewpoint 1 to 2 km off sees clear, as a fall in a gorge, is looked at from 150 m down its valley. Start-up lists them; `--map` numbers them; `--why=` names the place a point is at or the nearest; the window's title shows it; `--place=N` puts the camera at its viewpoint (with `--shot=` and `--weather=0` for a picture). `--survey` prints what the island holds (each community, rock and soil, absent, everywhere or in how many 1 km squares; features; extremes) and every candidate ranked with its score, then exits. Change what earns a name by a row's `worth`, not by a branch.
- What a block looks like is a tile in `assets/blocks.png`: a row for each material in the order of `Mat`, its top then its side, 16 pixels square, compiled into the game. The tiles are an artist's, from the Pixel Perfection pack in `assets/pixel-perfection/` (CC BY-SA 4.0; `assets/LICENSE.md` has the attribution, which must stay). `tools/blocks.py` builds the atlas: its `TILES` table names the tile each material takes, and each is recoloured to the material's colour in `MATERIALS` (run it from the repository root with a Python that has Pillow; with a file name it also writes an enlarged sheet to look at). After adding a material, name its tile there and run it, or the game stops at start-up. Do not generate tiles in code: the owner rejected that twice (`docs/interesting-world/notes/block-textures.md`). The plants are those of the warm islands of southern Japan (`docs/interesting-world/notes/real-vegetation.md`): judge a change to them against that note.
- Everything in a region is stored relative to its middle, and `--origin=x,z` places island 0's middle in the world (default 0,0), the archipelago moving with it; the generator's entry points (`gen_columns`, `gen_volume`), the start and each island's rivers and falls, drawn as a chunk is, relative to the eye, convert. Positions given to `--above`, `--pos` and the bench's printed end point are in the world.
- Judge a change to the land from the map first: `build/odincraft --map=out.png` forms the island (`--island=N`, default 0), writes a 1024-pixel relief map with rivers and exits, in about a second.
- `./build.sh` builds with unused-code checks on. `./build.sh run` plays.
- A run with `--shot` or `--bench` opens no window and takes no focus: it draws unseen, at full size, and a bench paces its frames as a 120 Hz display would, so its "frame interval" line can be trusted whatever else is on screen. A run with neither, started without a terminal (as an agent's is), stops with a message and opens nothing. Never add `--show` (which opens the window) unless the owner asks to watch: the owner works on this machine, and several agents may be testing at once. Benches run at the same time slow each other, so repeat one that fails before believing it.
- Look at every visual change yourself before showing it: `build/odincraft --shot=out.png` waits for the world to load, writes one 2560 × 1440 frame and exits. Add `--fly`, and place the camera with `--above=x,height,z` (height over the ground) or `--pos=x,y,z`, `--look=yaw,pitch` in degrees and `--hour=`. The region is 8,192 m square around its origin, and the default raised island spans about -2,600 to 2,950 in x and -2,300 to 2,700 in z of it; the map shows the whole region at 8 m a pixel with -z at the top, and yaw 0 looks toward -z. Other kinds and seeds put the land elsewhere: read the map first. The PNG is uncompressed; shrink it with `sips -Z 1600 -s format jpeg` before reading it.
- A fault that shows only while moving needs a frame from mid-flight: `--bench=30 --fly --speed=200 --pos=... --shot=out.png --after=3.3` saves the frame at that second without waiting for the world to load.
- Check frame pacing after any change to drawing or streaming: `build/odincraft --bench=15 --fly --speed=120 --above=-900,60,1700 --look=90,0`, which crosses the default island's south flank over forest. The target is no frame over 12.5 ms. Run it from the air as well, `--bench=10 --fly --speed=60 --pos=500,700,-1000 --look=200,-35`, which looks down through the cloud layer: the clouds are drawn once a frame at half size (`fs_clouds`), and the finest levels of detail are dropped when no ground is near enough to need them (`clearance`, `world.odin`). A bench also prints the faces and ground plants drawn a frame. Without `--fly` the same option runs along the ground, which exercises collision, and prints how far it got. After any change to how islands are held, formed or streamed, fly from the default island to island 1 across the sea: `--bench=40 --fly --speed=250 --pos=2000,150,-500 --look=82,0` leaves the east shore, crosses 9 km of open sea and ends 600 m off the cone's west shore; and run the forest flight with `--near=0`, which forms the other islands in the background while it flies (with the stand-in's two they are done within the two seconds a bench skips; with more islands the flight measures forming beside play).
- After any change to the land, the rock or trees, fly fast across the steepest ground of each kind, since steep land finds bugs gentle land hides: `build/odincraft --bench=20 --fly --speed=200 --above=0,80,-2000 --look=180,0` crosses the default island from north to south over its old sea cliff and upland, and `--kind=` with a path read off the map, or through the tallest face the start-up lines name, covers the others: for example `--kind=crater --seed=3 --bench=20 --fly --speed=200 --pos=-1400,330,30 --look=90,0` crosses a crater rim and floor, and `--bench=10 --fly --speed=100 --above=-300,40,-700 --look=95,0` crosses the default island's slide and its lakes. Over the sea `--above` measures from a sea floor 200 m down; start a flight or a view from the sea with `--pos`.
- After any change to the player, trees or the start, run `build/odincraft --bench=8` with no position: that is how the owner starts, on a river bank that no lake has drowned, on gentle ground with nothing in the three strides ahead to fill the first view, with no canopy tree within 6 m and no small one within 3 (half that where every bank is wooded closer); near 1,960, -300 on the default island, in open valley forest in the east (a bank by a fall, a cascade or in a gorge is never chosen), and about 43 m is the expected distance.
- The start-up lines print the island's kind, the bearing its swell comes from and its history, its rock, how long its land took to form (keep it near one second; it reads longer than alone, since the near islands form beside it) and how long a step cut at the coast takes to climb to the farthest stream head; the years the land has had on its finest cells against that says how finished it is. The last three lines are the figures the real islands were measured by, each beside the same figure on the real islands the kind is after (`after` in its row, `REAL` in `land.odin`), with a ! where ours lies far outside theirs; a kind after no real island (worn) is a guess and prints none. They end with livable ground (the `livable` overlay paints it), sheltered landings and the deepest closed hollow: judge a change to the land by them.
- A dark or odd patch in a screenshot is usually real geometry lit truthfully. Move the camera to it before changing the mesher.
- Place cameras with `--above`, not `--pos`, near the ground. A camera inside the ground sees sky through the terrain and looks like missing water or missing land.
- Water runs into a hole at a walker's pace (`RUN`, `water.odin`: 1.8 m/s down a drop of one in one), so a dug channel can be watched filling. Water takes time to move, and its animation drifts over time. Test it late and with numbers: `--after=45` delays a screenshot, `--dig=x0,y0,z0,x1,y1,z1` clears a box of blocks once the world has loaded (repeatable; `--after` then counts from the dig), and `--probe=x0,z0,x1,z1` prints ground, water depth and whether each column is supplied when the game exits. Probe across a river first to find its banks.
