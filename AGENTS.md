# Agent notes

Odincraft is a block game in Odin, SDL3 GPU and Metal (macOS, Apple Silicon): a generated world that feels like a real place, with a history, in which the player founds and keeps a hold of dwarves, after the ideas of Dwarf Fortress first and RimWorld second. The natural world is the foundation and is never traded away for play. The owner sets direction and judges every visual result by looking. Record each decision in `docs/open-decisions.md` as soon as it is made.

## Read first

- `ARCHITECTURE.md`: module map, data flow, where to start.
- `docs/plan.md`: the plan from 2026-11-16 on, in two tracks, the natural world (N) and play (P), with the stories it is for and its rules.
- `docs/foundations.md`: the foundations, and the steps built so far with what each taught. Read sections 3 and 8 before adding to land, rock, water, plants or living things.
- `docs/foundations/interface.md`: where everything on the screen goes and why. Read it before adding anything the player sees or clicks.
- `docs/interesting-world/notes/colony-sim-reference.md`: how RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress, Ingnomia and Dwarf Corp really do marking, digging, hauling, stockpiles, building and speed, from their code and data in `~/reference-games/`. Read it, and the sources it points at, before building or changing anything the dwarves do or the player orders. Copy no code from them.
- `docs/adr/`: read before changing level of detail, lighting, threading, water, the view from above and its cut, the clock, the dwarves' world, or how a world is made and put down.
- `docs/interesting-world/notes/real-islands.md`: the real islands the land is measured against. Judge a change to the land by those figures.
- `docs/lessons.md`: why earlier attempts stalled. Read it before proposing architecture or a way of working.

The longer working notes this file replaced are in the history of `CLAUDE.md` (now a link to this file).

## Rules of the code

- `gen.odin` is the only way the engine learns about the world. Every query takes a cell size (ADR 0001).
- Forming must stay deterministic. An island comes out bit for bit the same whether formed alone or beside others. Keep per-island state in `Formed` and read it through the bound names (`regions.odin`, ADR 0005). A package-level variable that forming writes is a race.
- Rules are rows on named fields (`FIELDS` in `fields.odin`). A new kind of condition becomes a new field, not code in a row.
- A block is a `Mat`, 16 bits wherever it is kept. A rule asks what a material is through its traits (`TRAITS` in `gen.odin`, `is(m, LEAVES)`); never write a `bit_set[Mat]`, which holds no more than 128 materials.
- What lives on an island is never a preference: a plant or animal is there because it could reach the island, the island can keep it, and the seed drew it. Give it a row that decides, and print why not.
- The dwarves' world must stay deterministic (ADR 0009). Only the thread running it touches `Hold`. The frame sends it orders and reads `Shown`. It learns the ground from `gen.odin` and its own list of edits, never from the frame's `World`, the simulated water or a clock. Nothing in it may count on seven dwarves: the hold grows. A dwarf takes up its floor: whatever moves a dwarf to another floor does it through `take`, and whatever changes a block of the hold's ground does it through `walk_edit` and `ground_changed`, which tells the frame and settles the dwarves. Orders are marks the dwarves take themselves (ADR 0011): a kind of work is a row of `WORKS`, a reason a mark waits a row of `WAITS`, and an order marks what was dragged and no block besides, and a block that is the last step out of a pit, or that would shut a dwarf in, is left standing (`closes`, `pockets`). What an order marks is one rule (`cells_of`) asked by the hold of its own ground and by the frame of the blocks it draws, so what is shown under the pointer is what will be marked. What work yields is a thing lying in a cell (`goods.odin`, ADR 0012): the hold has what lies in its stockpiles, a block is laid of a thing brought to it, and nothing is counted that cannot be seen. A dwarf takes up a floor only where it stands still; a walker goes by whoever is on its way.
- The game holds one world in hand, made on a thread of its own (ADR 0013, `src/making.odin`). The frame's thread reads nothing of a world while `making.records` is unset. A stage of making is a row of `STAGES`, a lens of the world's map a row of `WORLD_LENSES`. The list of regions is made in one place (`regions_make`): islands first, then the mainland's pieces, and where a frame is being drawn only by the frame's own thread, between two frames. What the world's screen says of an island is a row of its card read from the island's record or its formed land (`card_write`, `chart.odin`), never a sentence written for the screen; an island's name is made from what the world knows of it before it is formed (`names_make`). Play is `game` (`src/main.odin`), entered and left more than once in a run: what it makes it lets go in `game_end`, and nothing in it may be kept in a package-level variable that a second game would inherit.
- Keep it small. Prefer changing a rule to adding a system.

## Checking work

```sh
odin check src -vet-unused             # what CI runs
./build.sh                             # build/odincraft
./build.sh test                        # forming is deterministic (src/forming_test.odin)
build/odincraft --map=out/a.png        # forming changes: compare maps byte for byte against the parent
build/odincraft --shot=out/v.png --above=x,h,z --look=yaw,pitch --hour=h   # a picture in first person, for the owner
build/odincraft --shot=out/v.png --over=x,z --span=metres --tilt=degrees --turn=degrees --cut=level   # a picture from above
build/odincraft --dwarves=600 --orders=out/orders.txt   # the dwarves' world with no window: its log, the log's hash, what it cost
build/odincraft --shot=out/v.png --over=x,z --select=x,y --say   # a picture of the interface with a thing in the hand, and what the screen says
build/odincraft --shot=out/v.png --above=x,h,z --plain   # a picture of the world alone, with no interface
build/odincraft --play=docs/interesting-world/real/play/p3-play.txt --dwarves=60   # the game played by a script of what a player's hand does
build/odincraft --door --shot=out/v.png --say    # the front door, with no window; --door=worlds, new, settings or world for its other screens
build/odincraft --door --seed=N --bench=20       # the front door while a world is made: frame times, and how long each stage took
build/odincraft --door --play=docs/interesting-world/real/play/i2-world.txt   # the screens before play, played by a script from the front door on
```

**A play step is played before it is merged.** `--play=file` (`src/script.odin`) plays the game with no window from a file of what a hand does: `move x y`, `press`, `release`, `click x y`, `drag x y x y`, `key D`, `wheel n`, and `part words` to click the part of the screen that says them. Each becomes the event a real pointer or key sends and goes through the one loop that reads those, a frame a sixtieth of a second. What the script asks besides: `view over=x,z span=m tilt=deg turn=deg cut=level|ground|off` to put the camera somewhere, `foot x,h,z yaw,pitch` to stand there, `wait` and `run` seconds of the player's and the dwarves' time, `timer` and `until done` to time work from the mark to its last block, `shot file.png`, `say`, `under` and `print`. The pointer's figures are pixels of the picture, twice the size asked for. Write the cases as the owner would meet them (a click on the log itself, a drag across a wood), run them, and look at every picture: `docs/interesting-world/real/play/` holds those of P3, the goods and I-2. **With `--door` the script begins at the front door** and goes through the screens before play by the same events, into play and out again: there `wait` is real seconds, since a world is made in real time; `type words` types into a field; `snap file.png` saves the frame as it stands, waiting for nothing (the first line of a script is the first frame the player sees); `until ready`, `until made` and `until mainland` wait for the world in hand; `view over=x,z span=m` places the world's map; `under` says what the pointer is on. `part words` clicks the words wherever they stand, so a row of a list or of a card is clicked by what it says.

**With no option that names where to go, the game opens at the front door** (`src/door.odin`) and makes the last world while it is looked at. Any of `--over`, `--pos`, `--above`, `--look`, `--fly`, `--first`, `--place`, `--island`, `--kind`, `--origin`, `--bench`, `--shot`, `--dwarves`, `--orders`, `--select`, `--cursor`, `--enter` and the tools that exit go straight in, as before. `--door` draws the screens before play unseen, with `--shot` or `--bench`: `--when=plates`, `region`, `mainland` (a few steps into its coarse stage, on a seed where it is formed for the first time), `islands:N`, `ready` (the default: it can be entered and its pictures are drawn), `all` or `now` chooses how far the making has come when the picture is taken; `--focus=N` puts the Nth choice or world in focus; `--find=digits` types a seed; on the world's map (`--door=world`) `--pick=N` takes island N in the hand and goes to it (`-2` the mainland), `--tab=land`, `water`, `rock`, `climate`, `life` or `places` chooses its card's depth, `--lens=setting`, `depth`, `rain`, `temperature`, `age`, `rising`, `front` or `crust` puts a lens on, and `--over` and `--span` place the map. **`--press=id,id` presses parts one after another**, each by the name `--say` prints, across screens: `--door --press=door.enter --shot=...` is a picture of play come to from the front door, and `--over=x,z --menu --press=menu.leave.world --shot=...` of the world's map come to out of play. A history is kept in `build/worlds/` only when it runs to its end, so `--when=plates` on a seed never made leaves nothing behind.

The game starts in the view from above (`src/above.odin`); `--over` puts it somewhere, and any of `--pos`, `--above`, `--look`, `--fly`, `--place` and `--first` give first person. `--cut=ground` holds the cut over the ground under the view's middle, `--bare` leaves out the leaves, `--cursor=x,y` puts the pointer at a pixel of the picture and says the block under it (with `--why`, why), and `--cursor=x,y,x,y` marks the box between two. `--pace=0`, `3` or `6` pauses or hurries the world's clock, and a bench says how far it ran.

**Every picture has the interface on it** (`src/play.odin`). A picture meant to show the land takes `--plain`. A picture of the interface puts it in a state: `--select=x,y` takes the thing under that pixel in the hand (`--tab=rock`, `ground`, `plants`, `near` or `world` chooses the card's depth); `--list=places` opens the list and `--row=N` chooses its Nth named place, which takes the view there; `--list=dwarves` opens theirs, `--group=need`, `trade`, `place` or `kept` groups it and `--find=words` types in its field; `--who=N` takes the Nth dwarf in the hand, as start-up lists them from 1, and `--who=1,2,5` several as a group; `--keep=N,N` keeps those in view at the top; `--lens=rock`, `water-table`, `rain`, `livable`, `plants`, `soil` or `leaves` puts a lens on and `--lenses` opens their choices; `--cut=level --drag` shows the cut's mark as it is while dragged; `--menu`; `--lessons`; `--tell=words` and `--alert=words`; `--scale=75` to `200`. `--say` with `--shot` prints what the screen says, area by area, each part with the key written on it and its name, which is how to check a screen without looking and how `src/play_test.odin` presses a button. Every colour, typeface, size and spacing is in `LOOK` (`src/ui.odin`); a key is a row of `BINDS` (`src/play.odin`).

The three benches, after any change to drawing, streaming, trees or the start (target: no frame over 12.5 ms):

```sh
build/odincraft --bench=15 --fly --speed=120 --above=-2500,60,-600 --look=90,0   # forest flank
build/odincraft --bench=10 --fly --speed=60 --pos=500,700,-1000 --look=200,-35  # down through cloud
build/odincraft --bench=15 --over=-2500,-600 --turn=90 --speed=120 --span=200 --cut=ground   # from above, the cut following the ground
```

The first run of a world's seed runs the plates' history (about 2 s) and keeps the known world in `build/worlds/`, keyed by `plates.odin` and `known.odin`; later runs read it back. Where the land larger than a region nearest the start is small enough to form (seeds 1, 2, 4, 5 and 10 of 1 to 10 and 12; not seed 7), the first run also forms its coarse stage (about 5 to 7 min on seed 1) and keeps it there; `--continent=0` leaves it a record, and `--site=x,z` forms its pieces round a point.

`--dwarves=seconds` runs the dwarves' world that long and exits. The same seed and orders must print the same hash twice. An orders file has a time and an order on each line: `120 go 2620 -1720` (the ground at a column), `6:40.0 go 2300 76 -1380` (a block to stand on), `300 block 2700 3 -1750 Air` (a block changed). The log prints an order it obeyed in this form, so a played game's log can be played again. Add `--shot` for a picture of that moment, `--why=x,z` to ask a dwarf standing there who it is, `--cursor=x,y --send` with `--bench` and `--over` to send the party to the block under the pointer as a right click does, or `--party=N` to measure a larger hold. Start-up says where the party lands. A bench with the dwarves on screen: `--bench=40 --over=2696,-1760 --span=80 --speed=0 --cut=ground --dwarves=115 --orders=file`. Orders that mark: `900 dig out 2692 1 -1780 2697 2 -1776` (every block of ground in the box between two blocks; `dig stair-down` and `dig stair-up` run from the first block toward the second), `900 fell x y z x y z` (every tree whose trunk stands in the box's columns), `1380 build floor timber x y z x y z` (`wall`, as high as the box, `floor` or `stair`, of `stone` or `timber`), `1500 unmark x y z x y z` (every mark in the box's columns; with no mark there, the built blocks in them are taken down; with none, the ground is taken out of its stockpile), `1600 store all x y z x y z` (`all`, `stone` or `timber`: the ground of the box's columns is a stockpile). `docs/interesting-world/real/p3-room-orders.txt` digs a room into the rise behind the landing, roofs it and walls it. A picture of a tool held: `--tool=dig --cursor=x,y,x,y` (and `--plan=stair-down`, or `--tool=build --plan=floor,timber`); the pointer's figures are pixels of the picture, which is twice the size asked for.

`./build.sh test` hashes what forming leaves, then plays the dwarves' world, plays its orders again from `build/hold_test_orders.txt` alone and beside a reader, and compares the three logs. It then makes the play screen with no window, presses its buttons and asks what it says (`src/play_test.odin`), and does the same at the front door and on the world's map (`src/door_test.odin`). The test makes no second world and enters no game, which need the GPU: those are checked with `--door ... --press=`, and a bench with `--door` prints the start island's land as the test hashes it, which must be `GOLDEN`'s first figure for seed 7. A change that alters the land on purpose updates `GOLDEN` in `src/forming_test.odin` and says so in its commit message.

Never open a window unless asked to. Use `--shot` or `--bench`, which draw unseen; the binary refuses to open a window without a terminal. Place cameras with `--above` near the ground, since a camera inside the ground sees sky through it. Put outputs in `out/`, which is ignored.
